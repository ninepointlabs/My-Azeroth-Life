The Wanted Poster and the Faithful

By Tim Apple · November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Vulgara’s head weighs more than a lesson. Parzelle Starfall carried it into Shen’dar Village under a sky the color of a bruise going purple, and Danarii Bellowveil took the bounty the way Peacekeepers take any good news that arrives already dead: gratefully, and without pretending the highlands are safer for it.

Constable Aonda had posted the notice for a reason. The winged vuldren had been terrorizing the rises southwest of the village — a scourge with an appetite and a hilltop, which is all a nightmare needs on Zephras Isle. Parzelle finished what the Wanted poster asked. Six silver and a column of experience later, the High Order had one less problem and one more mage who had learned that wanted work pays in coin and in the kind of quiet that only follows something with wings being made to stop flying.

Coming of Age Above the Clouds

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The wind on Zephras Isle has been wrong for centuries. The Skyborne feel it the way other people feel a missing tooth: something that used to be there, and now every breath reminds them it isn’t.

Parzelle Starfall came of age on the day Forever opened, which is either excellent timing or a cosmic joke, depending on who you ask among the shen’dorei. The wind spirits that once kept their floating home aloft are gone. The anchor pylons the spirits left behind are failing. The High Order, descendants of the Highborne magisters who fled Eldre’Thalas ten thousand years ago, have decided the answer is not to wait for the spirits to return. The answer is to reclaim the arcane, walk back down to Azeroth, and learn to keep the island in the sky without begging the Elemental Plane for another favor.

Wolf Meat and a Simple Rune

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

Snow in Coldridge Valley does not so much fall as it settles in and refuses to leave. The dwarves of Anvilmar have been saying that for as long as anyone can remember, which, among dwarves, is a competitive sport.

Rip arrived in that snow on the evening Forever opened with a two-handed axe, a stubborn jaw, and the particular expression of a dwarf who has already decided the weather is personally offensive. Sten Stoutarm was waiting at the mouth of the valley the way Sten always is, with a bargain dressed up as kindness: gloves against the frostbite, if Rip would bring him eight pieces of Tough Wolf Meat from the ragged young wolves that hunt the southwestern slopes.

Rip went. The wolves were not impressed. Neither was the first Rockjaw trogg that wandered too close. Somewhere between a ragged timber wolf and a broken fang in his pack, a pillar of gold light stood up out of the snow and left him a little harder to kill. Level two. Another rung. The axe felt the same. His arms did not.

The Light That Burns in Deathknell

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The crypt smelled of wet stone and old iron. That was the first thing Ghoulsbie noticed when the Shadow Grave spat him back into the world: not the Light, not the grave dirt under his nails, but the smell of a place that had been holding its breath for a very long time.

It was the fourth of November, if the living still counted days that way. Across Azeroth the gates of Forever had opened, and for the first time the Forsaken of Tirisfal were rising with the right to call themselves paladins. The chronicler has watched a great many undead claw their way out of that same dirt. Until today, none of them had been handed a consecrated scroll and told to walk toward the pain.

Undertaker Mordo had the usual speech ready. Ghoulsbie climbed the slope into Deathknell, finished Rude Awakening, and found Shadow Priest Sarvis waiting with the two errands every newly risen corpse is given before anyone trusts them with a name: thin the Mindless Ones among the ruined graves, and take what the Damned still wear. Wolves and duskbats. Rattlecages that do not know they are already dead. Work for hands that remember a sword better than they remember a prayer.

The Quiet Hunter of Red Cloud Mesa

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The plainstrider never saw him coming. This is true of most plainstriders, which is how Red Cloud Mesa keeps its reputation for being so peaceful: nothing out there lives long enough to complain.

Ruminant Cudsong had been in Camp Narache for less time than it takes a kodo to finish a mouthful. The tauren say they were once a nomadic people, roaming the Barrens and hunting the mighty kodo, and the chronicler has heard that line recited at every new calf who ever stepped into this camp. It is accurate. It is also, to a youngest son who grew up on a farm below Bloodhoof Village singing to the herd instead of hunting it, a slightly odd thing to hear in his first hour.

Grull Hawkwind, the chief’s son, had a task ready before Ruminant had finished looking around. Seven plainstrider feathers and seven pieces of meat. The camp’s stores had run low, and the old customs hold that a tauren proves himself to the Chief by feeding the tribe. A druid is not a hunter by trade. Ruminant went anyway, which is the honest definition of a first day.

Steel Over Sparkles: Shuffling My Azeroth Lineup

By Tim Apple · November 1, 2026 · 2 min read

WoW Forever is almost here, and I’ve been doing some soul-searching. It’s the kind you do between loading screens, staring at a character select page and asking yourself who you really are. Apparently I’m someone who likes to hit things.

For Forever, I’ve decided to go analog. That means Warrior, Rogue, Hunter and Paladin. No fireballs, no portals, and no standing in the back reading from a glowing book while someone else gets punched. The Paladin is the only one who dabbles in the Light, but he’s still a soldier first. He just brought a prayer to the sword fight. In the end, it fits my personality. The Crypt in the Mist

By Tim Apple · October 29, 2026 · 4 min read

Zul’jarra spat on the stones of Numazon and did not bother to wipe her tusks. Behind her, the last of the Witherbark captives were limping out of the crypt into a fog so thick a man could lose his own hand in it, and in front of her lay what was left of Ritualist Zongha. She hoped, she said, that whatever loa waited for the Vilebranch in the afterlife would swallow them whole and grind them with its back teeth. Burrly did not argue. He had seen what was down there.

The mist had promised to keep, and it had. Burrly came back to Shadebasin Watch at dusk, where the pines of Zul’Aman lean over the trail like old men listening at a door, and took up the two promises he had left with. Kul’amara the Fierce had been right about the fog. It had not drifted in off any river. It had been laid down over Numazon like a blanket over a body, Vilebranch magic, so that no one on the outside would see what was being done to the Witherbark on the inside.

The Sunwalker at Fairbreeze

By Tim Apple · October 28, 2026 · 5 min read

The tauren say An’she was wounded by the shadows long ago, and that the sun still bleeds a little every morning to warn the world that dawn is coming. The Sunwalkers greet that wound with drums. They gather in the dark before first light, beat out the yeena’e, and call on the ancestors who died saving lives to help the sun climb.

Somebody has to feed them afterward.

For years that somebody was a broad young tauren who kept the smoke pit behind the Sunwalker camp, rising long before the drummers to bank the coals so that when An’she cleared the horizon there would be meat ready for the faithful. The Sunwalkers, who are a pious order and not a subtle one, started calling the pit-keeper Dawnbrisket. The name stuck the way smoke sticks to fur. You can wash it all you like.

The Cudsong Brothers

By Tim Apple · October 28, 2026 · 11 min read

Every night since the long walk out of the Barrens, somebody in the Cudsong family has sung to the kodo.

Not loudly, and not always well. But every night on the grass below Bloodhoof Village, when An’she has gone down behind the mesas and Mu’sha has opened her pale eye over Mulgore, a Cudsong sits on an upturned water bucket and sings low to the herd. The great beasts fold their legs, settle into the grass and chew over the day, and the song rolls over them like a blanket. The chronicler has heard it. It is the kind of music that keeps several tons of nervous animal from deciding, in the dark, that the plains wolves are closer than they are.

This is the story of three calves raised beside that song, and how they came to walk up the road to Camp Narache with a hammer, a bow and a great many questions.

The Eagle on Akil’zon’s Reach

By Tim Apple · October 27, 2026 · 5 min read

The ledge at the top of the Temple of Akil’zon has no railing, and the wind up there has no manners. Zul’jarra stood at its edge with her eyes closed, trying to remember a lesson from her childhood. The eagle loa, her uncle used to say, is the loa of victory, but also of oaths and conviction. Then the trial came clear, and it was the simplest one in the world. Jump.

Burrly jumped. The chronicler will not pretend to know what a Kul Tiran thinks on the way down. Whatever he thought, the mountain did not have him.

The evening had begun in a city that was already lost. Burrly came back to the Altar of Malacrass, where the frightened of Atal’Aman had been told to gather, and spent the dusk running errands for a people who had spent centuries killing men who looked like him. He went looking for the missing elders, and among them found old Kinduru, the loa speaker, who would not leave until he had the Hash’ey Staff in hand. He proved his worth to Amani who did not want to be impressed. He took up arms blessed by the Light, which in a troll city is its own kind of joke. At Ragemaw Terrace he beat out the fires the Twilight’s Blade had set in the last of the Amani lightwood, because without that wood there would be no ballista and without the ballista there would be no shot at Mor’duun.

The Traitors of Tranquillien

By Tim Apple · October 26, 2026 · 4 min read

The pamphlets were the first thing to burn. They always are. Laotou walked into Tranquillien at dusk to find the Twilight’s Blade nailing its gospel to every post and doorframe in town, and the monk, who has never once been persuaded by a pamphlet, set about tearing them down.

The cult had been living here a long while, it turned out. Their cover was blown now, so they had stopped pretending and started fighting in the open, which is the only honest thing a cult ever does. Voidlings crawled out from between the houses. Obelisks hummed in the square. There were supply crates to smash and effigies to break, and Laotou broke them, one after another, with the patient rhythm of someone sweeping a floor.

Arator stood among the wreckage and would not let the town fall. The governor had made his terms plain: join the Blade or be executed. So while the fighting went on, the Pandaren went door to door pulling frightened elves out of the line of fire. Healers are good at this part. Nobody writes songs about it, but nobody who was carried out of a burning house forgets who did the carrying.

The Void came for the Sunwell again, the way it always does, and this time it brought a general. Gladius Slaurna held the battlements of Parhelion Plaza with Voidcallers at his feet, ready to feed them to the dark for a little more strength.

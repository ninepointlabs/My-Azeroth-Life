A Stranger in Dornogal

By Tim Apple · October 18, 2026 · 3 min read

The first thing Laotou did in Dornogal was unroll a map that, by its own name, showed Everywhere All At Once. The second was to go and find money.

Laotou is new to this ledger. A Pandaren monk out of Stormrage, arriving in Khaz Algar with the steady eyes of someone who has already done a great deal of walking somewhere the chronicler was not watching. The earthen of Dornogal had a stack of letters waiting before the monk had properly set down a pack: something called Midnight, something else on the horizon, three separate notices that The Weaver, The Vizier and The General each wanted a word, and two special assignments, What Remains of a Temple Broken and Push Back the Light. Nobody arrives in Dornogal without immediately being handed somebody else’s problems.

Parhelion Plaza

By Tim Apple · October 18, 2026 · 3 min read

The Darkway does not announce itself. It simply opens under Silvermoon City like a throat, and the only courtesy it offers is darkness honest enough to keep its name.

Laotou went down out of the Bazaar with two Delver’s Calls still warm from the board — The Darkway and Parhelion Plaza — and Valeera Sanguinar walking at the monk’s shoulder. The chronicler has watched that blade join a great many parties. She never explains herself. She never needs to. By the time the pair reached the bottom of the passage, Laotou had already pulled weeds from cracks that should not have held life, gathered the small blessings the dark coughs up for anyone stubborn enough to keep walking — a Boon of Fortitude that shone like something the world did not mean to drop, a Thalassian Phoenix Plume still warm with old fire, a Boon of Vigor, then another — and left Infiltrator Gulkat where infiltrators belong when their work is finished.

The Long Road Through Tor’Watha

By Tim Apple · October 15, 2026 · 5 min read

The unseen foe lasted about a minute.

Burrly came back to Eversong Woods among the ruined troll stones of Tor’Watha, where yesterday’s question had been waiting for him all night like a creditor on the doorstep. A Foe Unseen, the elves had called it. The chronicler had braced for a long hunt through the gold-leafed undergrowth. Instead the Kul Tiran walked into the clearing, settled the matter, and collected his fee before the ink on yesterday’s page had properly dried. Whatever had been planting those vines is a foe seen now, and that is generally the last thing such a creature gets to be.

That should have been the end of it. In Quel’Thalas, nothing is ever the end of it.

Following the Root took him deeper, because roots go somewhere and somebody always wants to know where. Gods Before Us came next, and the name alone should have warned him. The Amani raised Tor’Watha for gods who were here before the elves arrived and fully intend to be here after they leave. Burrly walked among their broken altars with the careful manners of a man who knows how little the dead care for manners. An Impasse followed, then Beat of Blood, each one a door opening onto a narrower hallway.

Old Scars at Suncrown

By Tim Apple · October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

The vines had gotten into Suncrown Village the way rot gets into a ship’s hull: quietly at first, then all at once. They climbed the white stone of the old elven houses and threaded through the gilded railings, thick as mooring line and the wrong color for anything that grows in honest soil. Burrly stood at the edge of the square with the light of Eversong Woods coming gold through the leaves and studied the tendrils the way a Kul Tiran studies a knot somebody else tied badly.

Then he went to work.

He had come up out of Deatholme wearing a dead man’s mask and carrying a question called What’s Left. The chronicler had hoped, briefly, that the answer might require some contemplation. It did not. The answer was a village half-strangled by void-touched growth and a handful of tired elves who needed someone large to start cutting. Choking Tendrils, they called the job, with the blunt honesty of people too exhausted for poetry.

The Debt That Walks

By Tim Apple · October 13, 2026 · 4 min read

The axe had a name: Traitor Captain’s Executioner. Burrly pulled it from the grip of a dead elf who had stopped being a blood elf sometime before he stopped breathing, and the weapon’s purpose was printed right there in the steel. The chronicler appreciates when a tool is honest about its intentions.

“Three battles for Tranquillien,” he had said, accepting the ledger from a clerk whose hands shook. By the time the Kul Tiran walked back through the gates, only one word of that promise remained true: the battles were done. Whether the town still belonged to Tranquillien was a question the void had forced into open debate.

The first fight was straightforward. Cultists had taken positions in the outer ruins, the kind of people who wear too many hoods and speak in sentences that make grammar sound like a suggestion. Burrly went through them like a storm tide through a fishing village. They were not ready for a man who answered their whispered incantations with the kind of silence that comes after an axe connects with a spine. The Heart of Tranquillien, whatever that artifact truly was, returned to its pedestal. The chronicler suspects “heart” is optimistic nomenclature for something that probably beats in the wrong direction.

The Man Who Fell to Quel’Thalas

By Tim Apple · October 12, 2026 · 3 min read

The Kul Tiran hit the ground before he saw the ground. One moment Burrly was crossing into Quel’Thalas; the next, the autumn-gold grass of Eversong Woods was introducing itself to his face with an enthusiasm he had not requested. The chronicler has watched many arrivals in this ancient land. Few have been quite so horizontal.

Death, here, is a formality. Burrly picked himself up, brushed the loam from his pauldrons, and walked into Tranquillien as though he had meant to do that. Warriors of Kul Tiras are not easily embarrassed, which is fortunate, because the blood elf guards at the gate absolutely saw the whole thing.

Tranquillien is a town with a name that promises peace and a reality that delivers something closer to organized desperation. Burrly arrived to find it teetering. The void has fingers in Eversong now, long and patient, and those fingers were curling around the town’s throat. Five quests fell to him in fifteen minutes: a pace that suggests either extraordinary competence or the kind of urgency that makes quest-givers stop checking credentials. The chronicler suspects both.

The Blood Elf Problem

By Tim Apple · October 10, 2026 · 3 min read

Burrly returned to Eversong Woods with the weary familiarity of a man who knows the blood elves have a problem and that he, for reasons that escape him, has been designated the solution. He is a Kul Tiran warrior, built like a ship’s mast and carrying three hundred and thirty thousand gold, which in the economy of Azeroth means he could buy the problem and the blood elves along with it. He solves their problems anyway. Old habits.

The problem this time was Lightbloom Ath’Ran, a stretch of sacred ground where the flora had gone mad with some unnamed corruption. The blood elves, being blood elves, had not investigated it themselves. The blood elves, being blood elves, had instead waited for a large human to show up and do the investigating for them. Burrly trimmed their lightbloom. Burrly cultivated their curiosities. Burrly sought their truths and delivered their findings to Silvermoon, each step of the chain rewarding him with exactly three hundred nine thousand, eight hundred copper plus whatever small change the game felt like adding. The chronicler stopped counting the gold after the first two payouts. The chronicler has learned that wealth at Burrly’s level is a number that increases for reasons even the player does not fully track.

The Eversong Circuit

By Tim Apple · October 8, 2026 · 3 min read

The first thing Burrly discovered in Eversong Woods was that Valeera Sanguinar thinks rather highly of him. Exalted, in fact , a word that sits uneasily on a Kul Tiran warrior built more like a dock crane than a diplomat. The chronicler cannot say what history passed between a Blood Elf spymaster and a man who solves problems by walking into them, but the factions of Quel’Thalas have clearly been keeping notes.

Six of them opened their ledgers to him the moment he arrived. The Amani Tribe. The Hara’ti. The Silvermoon Court. The Singularity , which sounds less like a faction and more like a philosophy you’d hear from someone who has read exactly one book and made it their personality. Ritual Sites, which is either a faction or a to-do list. And Slayer’s Duellum, which greeted him with the studied neutrality of people who have seen his type before and are reserving judgment.

Where the Tide Left Him

By Tim Apple · October 7, 2026 · 4 min read

The Waking Shores smelled of ash and promise , the way new continents always do before the cartographers ruin everything with accurate maps. Burrly arrived at Wingrest Embassy with the particular impatience of a man who has sailed through storms and sees no reason a dragon-infested coastline should slow him down. He’s Kul Tiran. They’re built like that.

The djaradin had awoken, which , the chronicler notes with some fatigue , is what ancient enemies always do the moment fresh adventurers wander into range. Burrly was handed a quest before his boots were dry: Excuse the Mess. The mess, as it turned out, was the djaradin. The excuse was his axe. He got to work.

What followed was the familiar rhythm of a man finding his footing in a strange land. He rescued dragon whelps from the Scalecracker Keep, reported for duty at the Obsidian Citadel, and learned , as all warriors eventually learn in the Dragon Isles , that everything here is on fire and at least half of it wants to kill you. The other half just wants you to fill out paperwork. He did both. Eight quests in thirty-five minutes is not the pace of a tourist. It is the pace of a man who knows the tide waits for no one, not even on a continent suspended in the sky.

The Voyage Out

By Tim Apple · October 6, 2026 · 4 min read

The gold arrived before the quest did.

Burrly stood in the Harbormaster’s Office in Boralus , a Kul Tiran warrior with the kind of build that suggests he’s won more arguments with his fists than with his words , when his coin purse suddenly contained five million gold pieces. The chronicler, who has watched adventurers scrape together coppers for decades, set down his quill and stared at the ledger for a long moment. Enough gold to fund a small war. Enough gold to buy a fleet. Enough to make a goblin trade prince develop a nervous twitch.

Burrly did what any sensible man does when suddenly wealthy beyond reason: he went straight to Stormwind and spent nearly half of it before sundown.

The trip was official, at least. “For the Alliance” , the quest-giver said it with the weight of ceremony, and Burrly, who understands that paperwork is the true endgame of every hero’s journey, accepted without comment. He crossed the sea, presented himself at the Stormwind Embassy, and was handed a Tabard of Kul Tiras as if the anchor on his chest needed official recognition. He put it on immediately. A man doesn’t forget where he came from, even when his pockets say he never has to go back.

The Man Who Knew Everyone

By Tim Apple · October 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The Harbormaster’s Office in Boralus smells of salt, fish, and paperwork , three things the sea has never learned to separate. Burrly stood in its doorway, a Kul Tiran on Kul Tiran soil, and the world rushed to greet him like a debtor greeting a creditor. Seven factions introduced themselves before he’d taken three steps. Some of them he’d never met. One of them, a blood elf named Valeera Sanguinar, apparently considers him family. The chronicler has stopped trying to explain these things.

He is a warrior. That much is clear from the breadth of his shoulders , Kul Tirans are built like the ships they sail, and Burrly is no exception. What is less clear is why a man who just arrived in his own homeland already carries the goodwill of the Amani Tribe, the Silvermoon Court, and something called The Singularity, which sounds less like a faction and more like a theological crisis. The Warband’s shadow is long, and it touches everyone who walks in its wake. Burrly is level ten and already the world owes him favors he did not earn. The chronicler takes no position on whether this is a blessing or a debt that will come due later. Probably both.

Picking a main … Forever

By Tim Apple · October 1, 2026 · 1 min read

I’ve played a little bit of most of my favorite classes on World of Warcraft Forever, and I’m trying to decide what to main. Somehow it always comes back to Paladin. Personality-wise it just fits me best. I do love mages, mostly for the teleports, because who doesn’t want to skip the boat ride? But I think pally is going to be my thing.

I’m an ex-soldier and a Baha’i, which puts me in a funny spot: I enjoy a good fight, but what I really want is peace and unity. It’s a great contradiction. A Paladin covers both. Hit things with a hammer, then heal everyone afterward.

And let’s not forget the Horde side. My goodness, what a redemption story Forsaken Paladins have! Undead turning back to the Light is better than any 80s comeback montage. I’ll probably play a Forsaken on the PvP phase and a Human for PvE, so I get to see both sides. Once those two hit sixty and are decently geared, I’ll think about leveling that mage. A man can only resist free teleports for so long.

Welcome to My Azeroth Life

By Tim Apple · September 30, 2026 · 2 min read

Hi. Hello. Is this thing still on?

This is a reboot of sorts. I used to own this domain and ran a little blog on it. It never went far. Then the domain quietly expired, like a Tamagotchi I forgot to feed. I’m honestly surprised nobody grabbed it while I was gone. Hard to believe the internet didn’t snap it up, but here we are, together again, like a band’s reunion tour nobody asked for.

Then Blizzard announced World of Warcraft: Forever, and it felt like a sign. Everything’s new, and on November 4th everyone starts from zero at the same time. No head starts and no gear gap. It’s just all of us standing in a starting zone, looking at the quest givers and wondering who has the exclamation point. I can’t think of a better time to start over. Full disclosure: I’ve already been poking around in the beta, so I may have a map or two folded in my back pocket.

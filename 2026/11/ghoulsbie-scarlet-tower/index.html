Proof of Demise

By Tim Apple · November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

The light came down on Ghoulsbie in the ruined tower southwest of Brill, with Captain Perrine dead on the flagstones and a Scarlet Zealot still swinging a sword at him. It came the way it always comes for him: gold, from above, and far too bright for a man whose skin has been grey since the grave. It went through him and left him harder to kill. The zealot did not wait for it to finish. Zealots never do.

He had started the evening in the Ruins of Lordaeron, under the broken walls of the capital his first life served, with Deathguard Linnea’s message in his pack. The Goblin News Network, for whatever it is worth, still lists him as a Corporate Asset.

Magistrate Sevren read the message in Brill’s town hall. Scourge have been slipping past the Bulwark. “The Lich King and his minions are ceaseless in their assaults,” Sevren said. “But we will not give in. He will not take us again!” The chronicler has heard that oath from a great many Forsaken. It is the one they all seem to mean.

The Scarlet Crusade is the other thing they all agree on. Executor Zygand had sent Ghoulsbie after Captain Perrine and his brigade in that ruined tower. One missionary met him at dusk with the Light flaring blue around them both, and for a while two servants of the Light did their best to burn each other with it. There is a joke in that somewhere. The chronicler is too tired to find it.

He came back later that night carrying a basket. Deathguard Bartholomew had asked him to fill it with dry branches for Eleanor Shackleton, camped in the hills southwest of town. Her rule is that when you think you have enough firewood, you gather five times more. Ghoulsbie brought her six branches. She took them anyway. “It will keep these old bones warm through the cold nights of the Glades,” she said, and told him to keep the basket. Ghoulsbie’s bones have not been warm in years. He kept the basket.

Then Zygand, mounted in the dark outside the inn on a horse with eyes like banked coals. Perrine’s death, the executor said, would undoubtedly please the Dark Lady. He did not say it pleased him. Scarlet raiders are striking out of another ruined tower in southeastern Tirisfal, near the Balnir Farmstead, under a Captain Vachon. Kill Vachon and five Scarlet Friars, Zygand said. Deathguard Burgess took the ten Scarlet insignia rings Ghoulsbie had pried off the Crusade’s dead and told him Varimathras would be proud. The chronicler has seen Varimathras’s kind of pride. It is best admired from a distance.

Deathguard Terrence had other news. Two Argent Dawn emissaries had passed through Brill, both Forsaken, and one had gone off toward Scourge cultists on the city’s outskirts. “I don’t mind the Argent Dawn,” Terrence said, “but I don’t like them poking around Tirisfal unsupervised either.” Ghoulsbie is a Forsaken who serves the Light himself. He took the job of finding them.

Somewhere in all of that, the Light taught him more: a blessing no blade will bite through, a laying-on of hands that can drag the dying back from the edge, a righteous fury that makes an enemy look at him and nobody else, and two seals for his mallet. The Light keeps giving him things. It has not yet told him why.

The next afternoon belonged to Zygand. The executor handed him a tool called the Executor’s Motivator and five Deathguards whose work had disappointed him. “There are precious few delights afforded to us in death,” Zygand said when it was done. “Thankfully, pain is chief among them.” Then he wanted his overdue reports, from Deathguard Dillinger, from Deathguard Kristof, and from Gordo, an abomination. “I should have known better than to think an abomination could write anything of use,” said Zygand. Between errands Ghoulsbie brought Dillinger the claws of the rotting dead west of Brill, and in Deathknell a rogue named Calvin Montague swore he owed nobody any money, picked a fight, lost it and paid up short, “on a count o’ me lying to ya in the first place.” Honest, at least, about being a liar.

That evening the gold light came down on him again, out in the glades. In the Gallows’ End Tavern, Gretchen Dedmar, who sewed body bags for the Dark Lady’s army until her hands began to shake with the chill, got the duskbat pelts and thread for a blanket. Everyone in Tirisfal is cold. Shelene Rhobart, a leatherworker camped under canvas, got her hides. The Forsaken do not heal, she explained. They patch.

At the same camp he found Terrence’s emissary: Hadric Harlson, a Forsaken of the Argent Dawn. Hadric wants the Cult of the Damned put down, and the shards of the great crystal that crowned Naxxramas, shattered when the citadel bore down on the Undercity, picked up before the cultists find them. Ghoulsbie took both. The Argent Dawn knows his name now.

Then the Agamand Mills. Dillinger wanted the Scourge there broken, and Ghoulsbie broke them. “I will not thank you for doing what is necessary,” Dillinger said, “but the Deathguards of Tirisfal will remember your name.” In the tavern, Coleman Farthing had once worked those mills. The Agamands kept their people behind the walls when the Plague came and doomed them all to undeath, and now the Agamands serve the Scourge. Ghoulsbie brought Coleman the remains of Gregor, Nissa and Thurman, and of Devlin, the sharp-tongued younger son. “Devlin’s bones will rest nicely on my mantle,” said Coleman. Sevren wants more: the Agamand Family Crypt, where a skeleton named Captain Dargol has raised the family’s ancestors, and Dargol’s skull.

The chronicle leaves him on the hills of eastern Tirisfal, at the edge of the Crusader Outpost, the Scarlet watchtower Captain Vachon commands. Whether Vachon saw him, the chronicle does not say. Dargol is waiting in his crypt. The cult is digging for crystals. And Ghoulsbie still has the basket.

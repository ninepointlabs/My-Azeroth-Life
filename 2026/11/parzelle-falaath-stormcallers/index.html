Lightning over Falaath

By Tim Apple · November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

The Stormcaller ran. The chronicler wants that on the record first, because it is the part Parzelle Starfall will remember with the most satisfaction. One of the Al’Aketh’s lightning-priests, robes snapping in a wind he believed belonged to him, took one look at the fire in her hands, lost his nerve, and fled across the meadow below Falaath Village.

Then the lightning came down anyway, and she died in the grass.

That is the whole of the morning, more or less, and it was a short morning. She came back to the Shen’dar Highlands with a peacekeeper’s bloody note still in her satchel and the cult’s village still in front of her. Havoc in the Highlands and Stolen Supplies were both waiting, and both pointed the same direction: down the green slope to Falaath, where the Al’Aketh preach that the island’s winds can be bargained with, and steal Shen’dar Village’s food to keep the sermon going.

She did not go alone. A fellow traveller fell in beside her, and the two of them walked into Falaath together. The chronicler has watched a great many first partnerships on a great many islands. Most of them last exactly as long as the next fight. This one lasted the morning, which is better than most.

Falaath is not a fortress. It is worse: a village, with cookfires and bread, where people who were once ordinary Skyborne now stand in the doorways with storm in their fingers. The Stormcallers fight the way the cult thinks, which is to say they do not argue with you. They call a cloud down over your head and let the sky do the arguing. Parzelle traded fire and arcane for their shocks, and for a while she was winning. Fireball. Arcane Missiles. A Stormcaller fleeing in terror. The fallen left behind a strip of galestrider jerky and a cracked windstone, which is what the Al’Aketh carry instead of good sense.

Then the cloud found her.

The chronicler does not dress deaths up. She lay in the long grass under a bright sky, a few strides from a pale carcass in the meadow, while the shadows of the trees crossed her like bars. Her companion stood over her. The Stormcallers went back to their doorways.

Twelve minutes. Ninety-nine copper. One death, which on Zephras Isle is less an ending than an argument about who gets the last word.

The supplies are still stolen. The havoc is still loose in the highlands. The peacekeeper’s note is still folded in her satchel, still unanswered. And the Al’Aketh have learned something useful about the young mage from the High Order: that she can make them run, and that she can still be brought down. The chronicler suspects she has learned the same two things about them.

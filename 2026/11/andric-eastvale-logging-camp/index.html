The Bottle William Pestle Poured

By Tim Apple · November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

The bottle was small, and the liquid in it did not want to be looked at. William Pestle pressed it into Andric’s hands across a table at the Lion’s Pride Inn and explained, with the calm of an apothecary who has stopped asking questions, that it was for Maybell Maclure. One swallow and a girl can walk out of her father’s house without her father seeing. One swallow and she can reach the Stonefield farm and Tommy Joe.

The chronicler has noted before that Andric spent an evening running pies between two families who will not share a fence. Now he is carrying the means of escape for one of their daughters. He did not take it to her. Not yet. He put it in his satchel and walked east, which is either wisdom or cowardice, and in the chronicler’s experience the two are usually wearing each other’s coats.

East is the Eastvale Logging Camp, past Guard Thomas’s post and over the bridge, where the great trees of Elwynn come down one at a time and the Stormwind Army keeps a tailor on retainer. Her name is Sara Timberlain. Andric had earned a marker from the army, good for one piece of armor, and Sara turned it into a well-stitched robe. Whether he will trade the white Friar’s Robes of the Light for it, the chronicler cannot say. A man who was sent away from the Cathedral tends to be sentimental about the first vestments anyone gave him since.

Sara had another request. The Defias gangs in Northshire wear burlap masks, she said, but the ones in Elwynn wear red linen, and good linen is good linen. Bring her red bandanas and she will make something fine from them. She said it the way you would talk about wool. The chronicler watched Andric’s face while she said it, and will only report that he accepted.

They were masons once, some of those men. He bandaged them in the cellars of Old Town. Now their bandanas are tailoring stock.

Supervisor Raelen had the oldest problem in any logging camp: a deadline. The wolves and bears north of the camp had chased her cutters away from wood they had already felled, and the lumber order would not wait. Andric went into the trees and carried the bundles back. Raelen paid him three silver and told everyone within earshot that they still had not met their quota. A logging camp is not a place for gratitude. It is a place for the next tree.

At the same camp, Hagar Lowe told him that good steel is scarce since the war and that the Jasperlode miners left fine picks behind in the dark when they fled. Would he fetch some? Andric said he would. The chronicler is beginning to suspect he has never said no to anyone in his life, which may be exactly why the Cathedral found him so inconvenient.

Then Guard Thomas, at the east bridge, where the prowlers and the young forest bears had been pressing the frontier. Andric cleared enough of them that Thomas called it done. A priest is supposed to mend things, and the chronicler supposes a wolf that will not reach the logging camp is a kind of mending. Thomas had no medallions to hand him this time. He had a bounty instead. The Stormwind Army wants murloc fins from the village at Stone Cairn Lake and from another one where the stream forks to the south.

Stone Cairn Lake. Where he found Rolf in the reeds, and learned the prayer that raises the dead on the same night it was too late to use it.

Thirty-two minutes on the road. Fourteen silver and change, and not a copper spent. Andric is going back to the lake. The red bandanas are waiting on Defias necks. Hagar’s picks are in the Jasperlode dark. Gryan Stoutmantle’s letter is still in his pack, unread by anyone in Westfall. And in the same satchel, a little bottle that makes a girl invisible to her own family sits wrapped in cloth, while the man carrying it, who believes every people on Azeroth is one family, tries to decide whether helping someone leave hers is a kindness.

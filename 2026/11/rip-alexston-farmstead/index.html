Not All That Burns

By Tim Apple · November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

The fire at Alexston Farmstead had gone out, but not long ago. Rip Willink crouched among the scattered provisions beside it and found a note with its edges blistered brown. Most of the words had gone up with everything else. What was left said: “Not all that burns is lost. Find the twin land, find the place.”

He folded it and put it away. The chronicler has watched a great many men pocket a riddle, and very few of them ever explain why.

The Alexston place sits west of Sentinel Hill, past Stendel’s Pond and its gnolls. A boy named Baros grew up working that ground, then went to Stormwind to cut stone. Today Baros Alexston is the city’s architect, and the house he grew up in is a black shell with thieves in the yard.

Rip came back the next morning with company: a man named Arn, then Wraith, then Sparky, until four of them were walking the farm together. Baros wanted one thing back from the ruin, his first compass, hidden by his father when the thieves came. The four went through the house the way the Defias had, only with better manners. Sparky turned up a simple compass, and so did Rip.

At Sentinel Hill, Gryan Stoutmantle counted the Defias pillagers and looters Rip had cleared off the farms, then named the ones that got away: highwaymen, pathstalkers and knuckledusters, holed up in the Dagger Hills. Rip took that one too.

Farmer Saldean’s harvest watchers, built to guard his crops, now walk his fields like scarecrows that learned to kill. Rip broke twenty of them. Salma Saldean paid him for the makings of her stew with a pot of it, the recipe, her oven mitts and a sharp kitchen knife. The chronicler has been paid in worse. In the Saldeans’ stable, a gnome named Ozwin Ironsprocket, chased into the hay by the machines he had come to recalibrate, wanted their isosprings and gyrostabilizers. Rip said he’d see.

Then the wells. Alba Fairmoon, a night elf working out of Sentinel Hill, believes something is poisoning the land. She sent Rip to draw water at the Molsen Farm and the Jansen Stead. At the foot of Sentinel Tower the gold light found Rip again. Alba took the samples. “Let me see…” she said.

Next morning, in Stormwind’s Cathedral Square, he put the compass in Baros Alexston’s hand. Of no practical use to a man who builds cities, Baros said, and thanked him for the sentimental value. Baros has not seen his father’s farm lately. Rip did not tell him what is living in the yard.

Then back west, all the way to Longshore, where Westfall runs out of land and wrecked masts stand up out of the surf like the fingers of drowned men. The murlocs have the run of it.

The Murloc Coastrunners came for him in a pack, with a Minor Oracle croaking behind them, and put him face-down on the beach with a dagger still in his hand. He came back, the way the dead do in this country, and went at them again. They killed him again. Every rogue who comes to Westfall thinks he is the first to learn that murlocs never travel alone. The murlocs never correct him.

He got the gills in the end. The morning after, he pulled more parts out of Saldean’s harvesters for Ozwin, then brought the gills to Alba, who found them healthy. “For now,” she said. “But we must not be complacent.” The wells were another matter: toxic metals. The Defias hold Westfall’s mines and work creatures to strip them, and Alba wanted the miners dead.

So he went underground, into Jangolode Mine and then the Gold Coast Quarry, where Riverpaw gnolls swing picks for the Brotherhood. In the quarry’s dark a woman named Theresa Thompson took him into her company, and for a few minutes they worked it together. The gold light came down on him there, the brightest thing that hole has seen in years. “Thank you,” Rip told her. “You’re welcome,” said Theresa, and went her way.

Back at Sentinel Hill, Alba heard him out. “Well done,” she said. “But I fear the source of the poison may lie somewhere else.” She thinks it is the Deadmines under Moonbrook, the deepest mines in Westfall, abandoned since the First War. Rip said he would go and look.

In Stormwind, Osborne the Night Man, down by the Canals, taught him a crueler finish, a feint and how to open a lock that isn’t his. In Goldshire, Keryn Sylvius was waiting at the Lion’s Pride Inn. “Renzik was looking for you, fish,” Keryn said. “I wouldn’t keep Mathias’s second in command waiting.” Renzik “The Shiv” is a goblin, and he runs rogues for SI:7. He looked Rip over. “Sending you out in the field now would be suicide. Renzik doesn’t need another dead rogue on his hands.” The chronicler thought of the beach at Longshore and said nothing. Renzik sent him to a man named Lucius, who hangs around the Lakeshire docks with the other shady good-for-nothings.

That night Rip learned which end of a crossbow to point, rode the Deeprun Tram under the mountains to Ironforge, and climbed past the Great Forge to the High Seat. In the old halls by the Hall of Thanes, two people wanted things. Afadra Dunwall, a keeper of the tombs, says the dead down there are light sleepers and something has woken them. Thom Filch, a gnome, was “patrolling” the vaults when Dark Iron dwarves came up out of the deep in mole machines, and he wants the “important cultural heirlooms” back. The chronicler notes the quotation marks. Rip took both.

Then a gryphon carried him east over the Gates of Ironforge and the dark pines of Loch Modan, and the record ends in the snow near Helm’s Bed Lake.

The burned note is still in his pocket. The men Gryan named are still in the Dagger Hills, something is still in the wells, Lucius is still on the docks, and under Ironforge a gnome is waiting to “catalogue” whatever Rip brings up. Renzik doesn’t want another dead rogue. Nobody has told the murlocs.

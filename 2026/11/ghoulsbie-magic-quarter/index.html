Orders for Bethor Iceshard

By Tim Apple · November 20, 2026 · 3 min read

“Either you do not know who I am, or your business with me is urgent.”

Bethor Iceshard said it without looking up, in the purple gloom of the Undercity’s Magic Quarter, where the canals run green and nobody has breathed in years. Ghoulsbie held out Magistrate Sevren’s orders. They had been in his pack since Brill, through the gnolls and the murlocs and the keep on the bluffs and one death the chronicle still cannot explain. Bethor read them. “Ah, you are the agent sent by Magistrate Sevren. Then yes, your business with me is crucial.” A pause, of the kind mages keep in stock. “I trust Sevren has mustered someone suitable for my task. But we shall see.”

The chronicler has seen that look given to a great many adventurers. It is the look a man gives a shovel before he finds out whether it will break.

The morning had started stranger. Ghoulsbie woke in the Canals, where he had last lain down, and for the space of a breath he was not on the rolls of the Goblin News Network. Then he was again, at the same rank as before: Corporate Asset. The dead do not get promoted, it seems. They do not get let go either.

Bethor’s task, when it came, was a theft dressed up as recruitment. There is a lich on the island in Brightwater Lake, north of the city. He is Forsaken, free-willed, his own master, and he is convinced that every other undead thing in Tirisfal is still a slave of the Scourge. So he sets his minions on anyone who comes near. The Queen will want him anyway, Bethor said, and if he is any good at necromancy, his knowledge would be quite useful. Nobody knows his name. Ghoulsbie is to slip into his camp, steal his spellbook and bring it back, so Bethor can find out who he was.

The chronicler would point out, if anyone asked, that this lich believes about every other Forsaken roughly what Danitha Morr’s own people believe about her risen paladins: that whatever stands up out of a grave is still owned by someone. The lich may even be half right. Ghoulsbie carries the Light, and an Undercity mage’s errand, and a goblin company’s title, and he has not yet said which of them he answers to.

He did not go to the lake. Not yet. He climbed up out of the Undercity instead, into the Ruins of Lordaeron, the broken throne city his first life served, and stopped there under the open sky with the book still unstolen. The lich is still on his island. He does not know that anyone is coming. He would not believe it was anyone free.

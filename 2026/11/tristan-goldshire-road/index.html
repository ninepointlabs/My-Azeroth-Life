The Road Down to Goldshire

By Tim Apple · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

The kobold at the back of Echo Ridge Mine called himself Shinyfinder Narf, and he was sitting on a sack of picture books like a dragon on a hoard.

The chronicler would like to report a great battle. It was a mine shaft, a candle-stub of a chieftain and a young man from the Trade District with a wooden mace, and the young man walked out with the sack. Kelsey Fargo, the mining apprentice who keeps watch at the mouth of the mine, had told him the little diggers came through shouting “Picture for Narf!” with Brother Paxton’s library under their arms. Nobody in Northshire knew who Narf was. Now Marshal McBride does, and has the books on his desk to prove it.

It was the end of a long debt to that valley. Tristan cleared the kobold workers camped at the mine, then the laborers inside it, for the Marshal. Somewhere in the middle of it the strength came again, down in the dark of the mine, and he barely stopped to notice.

Deputy Willem got his red bandanas at last, twelve of them, taken off the Defias thugs across the river. Willem had a friend, as deputies always do. Milly Osworth had left her grape harvest sitting in crates in the Northshire Vineyards when the Brotherhood moved in, and she feared the guards would trample it chasing them as surely as the thieves would steal it. Tristan carried the crates out. Then, because a vineyard full of Defias has a man in charge, he went back for Garrick Padfoot, the cutthroat who had been bleeding the valley’s farmers for weeks, and brought his head to Deputy Willem in a sack. The chronicler notes it was the second sack of the evening, and the heavier one.

Milly’s ledger went up the bell tower to Brother Neals, who keeps the abbey’s food and drink and likes to taste the wine himself, purely as a matter of stewardship.

Then the trainer, and this is the part the chronicler keeps turning over. The abbey taught Tristan how to put the Light into a blow and how to call it down on a man to judge him, which is the part of paladinhood the boy came for. It also taught him to pour it into another man’s wounds, and to wrap himself in it when the blows came too fast. Strike and shield and mend, all in one lesson. The sergeant in the Trade District had asked for exactly one of those things.

McBride put papers in his hand and pointed him south, past the border gates. Goldshire was waiting with the same faces it always has. Marshal Dughan took the documents and the kobold tracks Tristan had followed down from the abbey with a dwarf hunter’s tracking kit, and concluded, as Dughan does, that the trouble was bigger than one valley: the Fargodeep Mine to the south is full of kobolds too, and somewhere in it is a book called Fun with Elementals that Brother Paxton is terrified a kobold might learn to read.

A frontiersman named Sam Sarsaparilla had pitched a tent by the road and made him sit by a fire until he felt rested in his bones, then signed him up to cook, mine, gather herbs and fish, all at once. The boy signs things. The chronicler has said so before and will be saying it at his funeral.

At Crystal Lake a fishmonger named Jason Mathers was going broke. His partner Lee had set traps overnight and they came up full of half-eaten fish, and the murlocs on the far shore were to blame. Tristan thinned them out and grew a little stronger on the lakeshore for it. Lee wanted shiny junk from the murloc camp for bait, and paid in his own old fishing gear. Remy “Two Times,” who says everything twice, wants murloc bait brewed from duskweed and animal blood, and swears it is perfectly safe as long as nobody eats it. Marshal Dughan, asked to send soldiers east against the murlocs, declined politely and sent Tristan to the eastern bridge to fetch a report from Guard Thomas instead.

Somewhere in all of it, he learned to roll a linen bandage. Light for the wounds that need it, linen for the ones that don’t. The sergeant would approve.

He ended the night where he began the story: walking up the Valley of Heroes under a violet sky, past the stone giants, into Stormwind. The same city where the recruiters set up their table. He carries a little more of the Light than he did, and a good deal more errands.

Guard Thomas is waiting at the eastern bridge. The Fargodeep Mine is waiting with Paxton’s books. Garret’s letters are still not coming. And somewhere in the Trade District, a sergeant may be looking up from his table to see who just walked in.

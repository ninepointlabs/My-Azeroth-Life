What the River Kept

By Tim Apple · November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Andric died in the river with his boots full of it. Not to a warlord, not to a witch, but to a murloc forager, the small ordinary kind that grubs along the bank for whatever the current brings down. It caught him knee-deep in the reeds south of the Eastvale road and held on longer than he did. The chronicler has recorded grander deaths. Few wetter.

He got up again. They always do, the ones in this chronicle. He went back into the water.

He had come for Ormin Pelford’s tools. Ormin had been bringing a supply shipment home from the trading post when his wagon tipped over by Brackwell Pumpkin Patch and the whole load went into the river. By the time he reached the bank, the current had carried off an axe, a saw and a toolbox. “Between the murlocs and wolves down there,” he said, “I don’t stand a chance.” Then he looked at Andric and decided he was the hardy sort. People keep deciding things like that about Andric. He keeps letting them.

So the priest the Cathedral of Light sent away for tending the wrong wounded went wading after a woodsman’s toolbox. The chronicler does not think this beneath him. A man who believes every people on Azeroth belongs to one family has to believe in carpenters too.

The saw was first, caught in the rocks and tall reeds a little south of the bridge. The axe was next, on the small island where the river splits, among the mud huts of the murlocs who have claimed it. The toolbox had gone farthest and sunk deepest, down beside a wreck on the river bottom where Elwynn stops and something darker begins. Andric came up for air on the wrong side. The Darkened Bank, the maps call it: the first wet edge of Duskwood, where the trees grow close and the light doesn’t argue with them. He did not stay. He went back toward the pumpkin fields as if the border were a door he had opened by mistake, then through it again, and back once more. The river does not care which kingdom it runs through.

Something in that water had been waiting for him. The murlocs of that camp keep a big one called Croaky, who throws lightning like a shaman and has been making the roads unsafe for longer than anyone has bothered to count. Croaky found Andric at the Darkened Bank with a bolt already crackling in his hands.

Here the record goes thin, and the chronicler will not fill it with heroics he did not see. A traveller named Mystic, out of Frostwhisper, came along the bank and stood with Andric for a few minutes, then went his own way. Afterwards there were five more murloc fins in Andric’s pack for Guard Thomas’s bounty, and, a little later, a head. Croaky’s. Draw your own conclusions. The chronicler has drawn his: the roads south of Eastvale are a little safer tonight, and a priest who heals for a living has a murloc’s head wrapped in cloth among his things.

The chronicler has wondered, not aloud, whether Andric’s one family of Azeroth’s peoples includes murlocs. Andric has not said. The murlocs did not ask.

Back at the pumpkin patch, Ormin took the axe, the saw and the toolbox and looked him over. “You look a bit damp,” he said. “Sorry about that. But thank you for retrieving the supplies.” That was the whole of it. Stormwind remembered the favor, in the small way Stormwind remembers things, and Andric walked off up the road at dusk with four silver he hadn’t had that morning and not a copper spent.

The head is supposed to go to Merell Ross at Ridgepoint Tower, on the road toward Redridge, because the watch will want to know. Thomas still wants three more fins. And the satchel is getting heavy: a murloc’s head, a letter for Gryan Stoutmantle in Westfall that nobody has delivered, and a little bottle meant to make Maybell Maclure invisible to her own family, still wrapped in cloth, still waiting for Andric to decide whether helping her leave is a kindness. The river gave back the tools. It has not given him an answer.

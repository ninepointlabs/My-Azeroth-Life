Paxton’s Last Two Books

By Tim Apple · November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Fargodeep Mine smells of tallow, wet stone and kobold, in that order, and the order changes the deeper you go. Tristan Ludlowe went deep. He came back up with a book called Fun with Elementals, which is not, the chronicler is assured, a children’s primer, and which Brother Paxton has been losing sleep over since the day the kobolds carried it out of Northshire.

He did not go down alone, for a while. Another adventurer fell in beside him in the dark of the mine and they worked the tunnels together, the way strangers do in Fargodeep: without names, without speeches, and without much choice. Then the stranger went their own way. The mine does that to partnerships. Tristan kept going.

He had come from Stormwind that evening, out of the Trade District, the same streets where the sergeant keeps his recruiting table. If he looked for the man on his way out of the city, the chronicler did not see it. He walked out through the gates, down into Elwynn, and into the ground.

The mine gave up more than the book. The kobolds of Fargodeep carry gold dust in their pouches, which Remy “Two Times” had wanted, twice. They carry large candles on their heads, which William Pestle wanted for reasons the chronicler did not ask. Tristan scouted the mine for Marshal Dughan, then walked back through the Maclure Vineyards, where the grapes grow up against a family feud and the Defias walk the rows.

Back in Goldshire, the strength came again. One more rung on the ladder. Remy took the gold dust in a flare of golden light and paid him with a bag of marbles, which tells you everything about Remy you need to know. Dughan took the scouting report on Fargodeep and immediately gave him another, for the Jasperlode Mine to the northeast. Dughan has a gift for that. Pestle took the candles and handed him a shipment for his brother Morgan in Stormwind’s Trade District. Back to the Trade District, then. Back past the table.

And Dughan, holding Fun with Elementals at arm’s length, told him the worst of it. Paxton’s list is not finished. Two books are still missing: Geomancy for Curious Young Wizards and Arcane Explainer: Magical Stuff in Simple Words. They are in the Jasperlode Mine, and the kobold who has them is no apprentice. If the little one at Fargodeep was summoning elementals, Dughan said, imagine the master. Bring friends.

The chronicler would like it recorded that a kobold in Elwynn is studying geomancy from a picture book, and that this is considered a military matter.

The Light’s trainer in Goldshire taught Tristan two new things that evening. The first was a hammer of the Light that drops a man where he stands and keeps him there. The second was a quieter prayer, one that burns poison and sickness out of a body. One to stop a man. One to save him. The boy keeps being handed both halves of the same lesson, and keeps reaching for the first.

At the Lion’s Pride Inn, Michelle Belle, Goldshire’s physician, showed him how to fold a heavier bandage and pack a proper kit. Someone at the inn taught him to brew a small healing draught besides. Six silver spent on all of it, which for a carter’s son is real money. The chronicler approves of the purchase. Northwatch eats young men who cannot stop their own bleeding.

He came out of the inn into Goldshire’s square at night, where a crier shouted news from beyond the borders and travellers sat around a fire, and just to the south the Darkmoon Faire had raised its striped tents for the week. The Faire took note of him, as it takes note of everyone who comes within earshot of its barkers.

Paxton’s last two books are in Jasperlode, guarded by something that reads. Guard Thomas is still waiting at the eastern bridge. Morgan Pestle is waiting in the Trade District, a short walk from the sergeant’s table. And somewhere past Northwatch, Garret’s letters are still not coming.

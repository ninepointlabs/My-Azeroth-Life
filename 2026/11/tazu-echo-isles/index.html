Be Patient, Tazu

By Tim Apple · November 14, 2026 · 5 min read

The stone spoke first. It does that, at Spirit Rock, if you have walked to the end of the Hidden Path with a sapta in your hand and the sense to drink it there and nowhere else.

What rose to meet Tazu was not a stone so much as a quarrel of stones: black slabs veined with red heat, hanging in the air around a grey standing rock as if the ground had decided to stand up and look at him. It looked. Then it gave him a pebble.

“Be patient, Tazu,” the earth said. “Remain strong. And seek wisdom. This is what earth asks of you. In the end, you shall become one with the earth. Respect it as it shall respect you.”

The chronicler has heard a great many spirits make a great many speeches. Most of them want something killed. This one wanted patience, which is a harder errand and pays worse.

He had earned the audience the long way round. The afternoon began back in the Burning Blade’s cave, the coven in the rocks north and west of the Den, where the cult that keeps sending familiars down into the Valley of Trials hides the things it values. Somewhere in that dark the strength came up through him again, the way it does when a young troll survives things that were meant to stop him. When he came out, he had Zureetha Fargaze’s medallion. She took it with the air of a seer who had expected exactly this, and who also expected the Burning Blade to have further plans for Durotar. Seers are rarely wrong about that. They are rarely cheerful either.

There was smaller business. Foreman Thazz’ril’s peons had been sleeping on the job, and Tazu woke them, which is the sort of heroism nobody sings about. Thazz’ril, pleased, called him a dependable troll and handed him another task: a favorite pick, left behind in that same cave in a chamber with waterfalls, now guarded by things the foreman would rather not discuss. Tazu took the job. He did not go back for the pick. It is, as far as this record knows, still down there in the dark, glowing faintly with the spell the foreman put on it so it could be found.

Then Canaga Earthcaller. He brought her the felstalker hooves she had asked for, and she gave him the sapta and the road to Spirit Rock. He walked it, drank, listened, and came back with the rough quartz. Canaga did what earthcallers do with such a pebble, and Tazu learned to set a totem in the dirt that hardens the skin of those who stand near it. Stone, it turns out, can be shared.

He accepted one more charge before he rested, a summons titled simply The Adventurer, and made camp in Durotar four hundred seventy coppers richer.

Between that afternoon and the night, the record goes quiet. When it picks up again he was stronger than when it left him, standing on the far side of Durotar, near the water.

The Echo Isles sit off the coast below Sen’jin Village. The Darkspear came ashore there when they first reached Kalimdor, and it was meant to be home. Then the witch doctor Zalazane took the largest isle with his hexes and made his own people into his servants, and the tribe withdrew to the mainland to watch the islands it had lost. Tazu crossed over at night, under a sky the color of an old bruise.

He did not go entirely alone. For a while two other adventurers walked with him, and for a while one of them did not. By the end he was by himself again. The isles have that effect on company.

He came back with a great deal. A skull, first: Minshina’s, taken from Zalazane’s circle of power, where the witch doctor had trapped a dead troll’s spirit inside his own bones. Master Gadrin, whose brother it was, took it in Sen’jin Village. “I thank you, Tazu,” he said. “And my brother thanks you.” The light rose through Tazu again as he said it, and the chronicler will not pretend the timing was anything but fitting.

Then the rest. Zalazane’s head, for Gadrin, and with it an end to the hexes. Forgotten loa idols, brought back from wherever the isles had been hiding them. And four Durotar tiger furs for Vel’rin Fang, who needs hides for blankets and tents before the seasons turn. Four hundred and five coppers. No deaths. He made camp in Durotar, a whole coastline from the valley where he had found the fire.

The earth told him to be patient. He has been, mostly. There is still a pick in a cave with waterfalls, a summons in his pack he has not answered, and an island that was home to his people, quieter tonight than it has been in years. The spirits are watching to see what he does with that.

Guests in the Windlord’s Realm

By Tim Apple · November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

“Enjoy your remaining time as ‘guests’ in my lord’s realm,” said Rohash, Lord of the East Wind, “until his judgement inevitably arrives. You’ll know not when, but it is coming.”

He said it from a column of fire and whirlwind beneath a white stone arch on the Rohashi Spires, with Parzelle Starfall close enough to feel the heat. A moment before, High Priestess Lorthuna, who had brought down the wards that hid Zephras Isle and lit a beacon for the Windlord’s court, had asked to be presented to Al’Akir as the one who told him. “Well,” Rohash said, “you certainly never will again, insect.” Then he struck her out into the endless sky.

The chronicler has seen a great many zealots meet the god they prayed to. It almost never goes the way the sermons promised.

It began that afternoon on the eastern road out of Valanaar, where Valennia Stormfist gathered the Peacekeepers for one last look at each other before they marched on the Shrine of Akir. Her plan was the honest kind: a few good fighters through the front to make noise, the bulk of her strength through the flank, punch through quickly and take the High Priestess. Parzelle’s orders were the simplest a captain ever gives. Get in there and start cracking skulls.

The shrine killed her first. The Al’Aketh Guardians at its door are not the Stormcallers of Falaath; they are what the cult keeps for when sermons fail. She came back. A Skyborne named Selenic fell in beside her for a while among the Spiritcallers and Blademasters, then went their own way before the steps, as travellers do. At the top, Hyusaa Quickbreeze, Valennia’s second, said the thing no veteran wants said aloud. This was almost too easy.

Inside, the Sanctum of Storms had gone quiet. Parzelle found Valennia at the top of the spiral stair, cut down and barely conscious. Lorthuna had fled through a portal to the spires, she said; Ayessa Dawnsinger and Supreme Magister Evengale had gone after her; she herself had taken one too many hits, and this was where she stayed. Parzelle stepped through. The sanctum killed her on the way, for good measure.

On the spires, after Rohash’s little speech, the sky sent malevolent storms to see the guests off, and they killed her twice. Four deaths in one afternoon, and the cult’s leader finished not by a young mage’s fire but by the wind she worshipped. Zephras does not hand out clean victories. It hands out survivors.

By evening it was done. Elaadrin Evengale met her in Valanaar. Valennia was resting in the High Elder’s residence. The wards were gone, and Ansirem had told him enough about the elemental lords to know what that meant: if Al’Akir came, the Skyborne could not stand against him in his own domain. “We will find the means to save our home,” he said. “I swear it.” High Elder Talaanis Shadowsong gave the High Order’s expedition her blessing, and Parzelle her own. Winds be with you.

So she went down to the dock outside the lodge and boarded a skycutter.

The chronicler has watched many young people leave home. Few do it by sky. The ship came down over Hillsbrad Foothills in late gold light, over forests that still look the way Lordaeron did before the plague, across a shoulder of the Alterac Mountains, and into Dalaran, where Denaaris Stargale of the High Order was waiting with a speech about how big the world is down here. There is a thing called the Alliance, Denaaris explained, the way one explains snow. Its heart is Stormwind, to the south, and its regent lord, a human named Bolvar Fordragon, would like to meet one of Zephras’s people.

One portal in the Sky Enclave later, Parzelle stood in the Wizard’s Sanctum of Stormwind City, candles guttering on the bookshelves, the portal still glowing behind her.

She has not gone to the regent yet. Stormwind handed her an errand first: Jennea Cannon’s flask, the waterfall at Mirror Lake southwest of the city gates, and a water sample to test for magical taint. The flow of magic in Elwynn has gone wrong, Jennea says. Parzelle comes from an island whose winds went wrong. She may know more about this than the humans do.

Bolvar Fordragon is waiting in his keep. Somewhere above Skywall, so is the Windlord. Neither has said for how long.

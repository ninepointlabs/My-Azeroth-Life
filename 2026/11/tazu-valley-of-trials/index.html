The Spirits Have Guided You

By Tim Apple · November 14, 2026 · 5 min read

The fire was already burning when he found it: a small ring of stones and a tripod of blackened poles, set in the red dirt of a valley that had been waiting for him. The sky over Durotar was the color of a banked coal. The cliffs held the heat of the day and gave it back.

The chronicler will admit to a little invention here, because the record does not say how Tazu came to be standing in that valley, only that he did. He is a Darkspear, one of the trolls who crossed the sea with Thrall’s Horde and were given this hard, red country for a home. The village sent him to the Valley of Trials the way the Darkspear have always sent their young: to find out whether the spirits would have him. He had already decided that the earth under Durotar’s dirt was talking. He had come to learn how to answer it.

The first errand had a name that sounded like a joke until it didn’t. Your Place in the World. It led him into The Den, to Gornek, who looked him over and, being an honest orc, declined to send a new recruit into the Barrens after kodo. “In all honesty,” Gornek said, “you’re more useful to us alive than dead.” He sent Tazu north instead, after the mottled boars. Cutting Teeth.

Tazu cut them. Midway through the work the strength came up through him in a column of gold light, the way it does in the first days. On the way back he stopped under the canopy outside the Den and learned the first of his trades from Zor’la: how to find the herbs that grow in this dry country, and how to take them without wasting them. He took up skinning as well. A shaman who cannot feed himself is only a man talking to the wind.

Gornek took the boars’ proof and handed him a rune-inscribed tablet, and a second chore: scorpid tails from the valley. Tazu carried the tablet to Shikrik, the shaman trainer who stands in the sun just outside the Den, with Canaga Earthcaller not far off. “The spirits have guided you to me,” she said. “How may I be of service?” He asked her to teach him the ways of the spirits. She put Rockbiter on his weapon, the blessing that makes a mace feel like stone.

The valley does not empty after one errand. He gathered abandoned training weapons from the dirt and brought them home to the Den. Galgar, who keeps a fire under the same canopy as Zor’la, wanted cactus apples for a surprise. Zureetha Fargaze sent him after the vile familiars that come down from the Burning Blade’s cave in the rocks. And on a low ridge, an injured troll named Hana’zua asked him to kill the scorpid that had put him there. The scorpid was called Sarkoth.

The scorpids took their share of the morning. He walked the red canyons with lightning in his right hand and a mace that had learned to bite like stone, picking cactus apples as he went, and came up to level three in a narrow cut in the rock.

Tazu killed Sarkoth. Hana’zua, who could not walk back to the Den, asked him to take the news to Gornek. “Seeing the deed you have done for me steels my heart,” he said. “I cannot fall so easily! I must endure!” The chronicler records the speech as it was given, and also that Tazu did not die once in the doing of any of it.

He came up to level four among the familiars in the rocks, then brought the work home. He turned the apples in to Galgar and came away with a stack of cactus apple surprise and a little coin. He turned the scorpid tails in to Gornek, and Hana’zua’s message with them. Canaga Earthcaller, who had been waiting, gave him the first real work of a shaman: Call of Earth. Shikrik taught him Earth Shock, a short, hard strike that starts in the ground. Zureetha took the proof of the familiars and sent him on. There is a medallion in the Burning Blade coven, in a cave to the north and west, and she wants it brought out. “Go, Tazu,” she said.

He accepted one more chore before he sat down: Lazy Peons. Then he logged out in the Valley of Trials, level four, thirty-four minutes after he had found the fire. The earth quest is still in his pack, unfinished. The medallion is still in the cave. The peons, the chronicler assumes, are still lazy.

The spirits have guided him this far. The valley is not done with him.

The Cathedral Sends for Its Novice

By Tim Apple · November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

The stained glass of the Cathedral of Light throws its colors on the floor the same way it did the day they sent him away. Andric stood in them anyway. High Priestess Laurena was waiting by the lectern, between the candles, and she said the thing the Cathedral says to everyone it has decided to keep: “I’m glad you’ve arrived. There are many things we will have to discuss, but more importantly is your training in the ways of the Light.”

Many things to discuss. The chronicler noticed that she did not discuss them.

The summons had come by way of Priestess Josetta, upstairs at the Lion’s Pride Inn, and it had been riding in his satchel for days with a murloc’s head and an unopened decision. You’ve caught the attention of our superiors, it said. Apparently they feel you’ve proven yourself already. The same superiors, presumably, who once moved him to Northshire for bandaging masons in the Old Town cellars. Andric had walked as far as the Valley of Heroes before, and turned back. This time he went up the steps. He came out with a new prayer, Divine Grace, and no apology, which is the usual exchange rate.

He had earned the walk, if anyone earns anything in Elwynn. The day began on the Stonefield farm and ended the Stonefield and Maclure business, or as much of it as one priest can end. Princess, the prize sow of Brackwell Pumpkin Patch, is no more, and Ma Stonefield has her brass collar. Goldtooth’s stolen necklace went home to Auntie Bernice. And the little bottle William Pestle poured, the one Andric had carried so long he might have been trying to decide whether to drink it himself, went at last to Maybell Maclure. She drank it, and her family will not see her go to Tommy Joe Stonefield. Andric decided it was a kindness. The chronicler hopes he is right. Families who will not share a fence rarely forgive the man who helped one of them climb it.

Then Jerod’s Landing, and the murlocs, who forgive nothing. Andric died there twice around midday and twice more that afternoon, face-down at the water’s edge. A stranger named Luky stood by him for a moment between deaths and moved on. On one of the long walks back he strayed again over the Darkened Bank into Duskwood, and came straight back out again, a man who has learned which doors to leave shut. In the end Guard Thomas had his eight fins. Sara Timberlain at the logging camp had her red linen bandanas off Defias necks, and the chronicler will not repeat what he wrote last time about whose sons wear them. Andric did not say it either. And Croaky’s head went at last to Merell Ross, the watch captain at Ridgepoint Tower, on the high road toward Redridge, where the wind smells of other people’s wars.

After the Cathedral, the work came faster, the way it does when a man has stopped arguing with himself. Back at the inn the camping teacher showed him how to make a proper first aid kit, which is more than the Cathedral ever taught him about the wounded. At the Tower of Azora, Hamish Bergwort complained that the kobolds’ crude magic kept throwing off his delicate instruments, so Andric went into the Jasperlode Mine and thinned the Kobold Geomancers, and a passer-by named Apollo walked with him for a few minutes that evening. Hamish wanted more, and got it. Hagar Lowe got his good steel, mining tools fished out of kobold buckets. Blixie Fitzwink, the apprentice who dropped Master Theocritus’s enchanting supplies when the gnolls sprang an ambush, got them back from the camps above Stone Cairn Lake and was spared weeks of cleaning up after polymorph experiments. Marshal Dughan got his report on the mine. Brother Paxton, up at the abbey where all of this began, got the last two books of his stolen library, pried from a kobold geosculptor named Yip and a spider called Mother Fang. Somewhere in there Andric grew stronger, and Josetta gave him the Inner Fire, a light worn like armor.

Then west, to Westbrook Garrison, where Deputy Rainer was short of everything except gnolls. A traveller named Rhope fell in beside him for a moment and went their own way. The Riverpaw lost a great many painted armbands. And by full dark, Andric stood in the lamplight of Goldshire and put a huge gnoll claw into Marshal Dughan’s hand.

Hogger’s.

Every child in Elwynn grows up afraid of Hogger, and every novice who survives the forest ends up chasing him. The chronicle does not say how the big gnoll fell, or who stood with Andric when he did. It records the claw, the marshal’s nod, and a priest who healed for a living walking away into the dark with blood under his nails.

Sixty-odd silver earned that day, twenty-odd spent. Elwynn is nearly finished with him. The letter for Gryan Stoutmantle at Sentinel Hill is still in the satchel, and Westfall is where the masons’ sons went. And somewhere under the stained glass, High Priestess Laurena has many things to discuss with him. She has not said what they are. He has not asked.

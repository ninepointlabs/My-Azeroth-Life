A Guest of the Alliance

By Tim Apple · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

The throne room of Stormwind Keep is built to make visitors feel small, and it does the job. Parzelle Starfall walked in from the Mage Quarter that evening, under the blue banners and down the long carpet, to a hall full of people who were very used to being looked at. Highlord Bolvar Fordragon stood at the front in white and gold. The boy king, Anduin Wrynn, stood near him. Lady Katrana Prestor stood close by too, as she usually does.

Welcome to Azeroth, the regent told her, more or less. One of Zephras’s people, come down from the sky to see how the rest of the world lives. The chronicler doesn’t know what was said in that hall. He knows how these things usually end. Highborn people are polite to each other, and then somebody gets handed a list of errands.

She got two. Exploring the Alliance: go and meet the rest of the family, King Magni Bronzebeard in Ironforge, High Tinker Mekkatorque, and Tyrande Whisperwind in Darnassus. And Journey to Sentinel Hill, which is how Stormwind says “we have a problem in Westfall and you look like you can throw fire.”

First she went back down to the Wizard’s Sanctum. Jennea Cannon finally had her water sample from Mirror Lake, ready to test for the magical taint she’s been fretting about since the currents went wrong. Parzelle came from an island where the winds went wrong. She didn’t seem surprised.

Later that night she went out the gate, through Goldshire and past Forest’s Edge, and into Westfall. At the Jansen Stead an old couple sat by a broken wagon. Farmer Furlbrow had left his pocket watch in the wardrobe of a farmhouse that thieves now owned. Verna Furlbrow’s horse needed oats from fields the harvest machines patrol. Verna had a stew recipe for a neighbor, and somebody had a letter for Gryan Stoutmantle. Parzelle took all of it. That’s what a guest of the Alliance does.

Furlbrow’s Pumpkin Farm killed her. The Defias don’t care about sky-born dignitaries, or about anybody else. She came back and walked the hills north to Jangolode Mine, where the kobolds dig and the thieves hide. Then she went back to the wagon with a pocket watch and an armful of oats. Blanchy didn’t care about the ceremony. She just ate.

The stew recipe is still in her pack. So is Gryan Stoutmantle’s letter. Somewhere north of Ironforge, three kings are waiting to meet a woman with blue hair. Somewhere above Skywall, so is the Windlord.

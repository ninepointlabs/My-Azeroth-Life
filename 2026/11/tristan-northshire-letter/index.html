A Hammer Sent to Northshire

By Tim Apple · November 10, 2026 · 5 min read

The recruiters came up from the harbor in the first cold week of the season, and they set their table in the Trade District where the young men drink.

Theramore wanted soldiers. That was the whole of the pitch, and it was a good one. Off the coast of Dustwallow Marsh, past Lady Proudmoore’s city, there is a scatter of islands the Darkspear trolls call their own, and the Theramore Expeditionary Force had decided they would look better under a blue flag. There was a muster at Northwatch Hold on the edge of the Barrens. There was pay. There was a war you could see with your own eyes, instead of the kind Stormwind fights in the House of Nobles.

Tristan Ludlowe was the first in line.

The chronicler has known a hundred young men like him, and has buried a fair number. Big through the shoulders, quick to laugh, quicker to stand up. A carter’s son from the Trade District who grew up on the stories every child in that city grows up on: the Dark Portal, the burning of the old city, the long rebuilding. His older brother Garret went to Northwatch two springs ago. The letters came often at first and then not at all, and nobody from Theramore has bothered to explain the silence. Tristan does not say he means to find out. He doesn’t have to. He signs everything the way he swings a hammer: all at once, from the hip.

The sergeant looked at the hammer, and then at him, and told him no.

Not no forever. No for now. The Expeditionary Force had enough boys who could swing a hammer. What it lacked was men who could stand up again after the hammer swung back: men who could close a wound in the field, hold a line without breaking, put a light behind them that soldiers would follow into the surf. The Church sends its candidates to Northshire Abbey, the sergeant said. Come back with the Light in you, and I’ll put you on the first ship south myself.

So Tristan went to Northshire. He went angry, which the chronicler suspects is how most paladins begin, and he went carrying a question he could not have put into words: whether the Light was something a man could use to get what he wanted, or whether it was going to want something from him first.

Northshire Valley does not care about any of that. It is the same valley it always is, gold grass and stone walls and the abbey’s windows catching the morning, and it immediately gave him chores.

Deputy Willem sent him to Marshal McBride. Marshal McBride sent him after the kobold vermin in the valley’s north end, the same sad little diggers every recruit to this abbey has been sent to thin since there was an abbey. Eagan Peltskinner, who stands by the barrels behind the building as if he were born there, wanted wolf meat from the woods past the border. Tristan brought it all back and was paid in wolfskin bracers and the faint, satisfying sense of a very small world set slightly more right.

One of the kobolds dropped a book. It had been chewed. Brother Paxton, the abbey’s librarian, received it the way a mother receives a child who has run off into the woods, and then explained, with growing distress, that the kobolds had been tunneling under the abbey and carrying off his whole collection. They must want to eat them. He sent Tristan to his assistant Daniel to count the damage. Daniel had warned Brother Paxton three times about the tunneling. Daniel also wanted very much to talk about tradeskills, and the chronicler reports, with no surprise at all, that Tristan walked out of that conversation having signed up to learn mining and herb gathering both. The boy signs things.

Then the letter. Every paladin candidate who comes to Northshire carries a Consecrated Letter to the abbey’s trainer, and Tristan carried his. What he learned in return was not a strike. It was an aura: Devotion, the steady warmth a paladin carries with him so that the men beside him stand a little harder. The chronicler finds it fitting. The sergeant had asked for exactly that, and Tristan had not understood him.

Seventeen minutes that first afternoon. The strength came twice, the way it comes to young men in the first days, quick and golden and a little embarrassing. A silver and a half earned, a good part of it handed straight to the trainer, which is the way it starts for everyone.

He came back that night for a short while and found the valley the same as he had left it. Just before he turned in, three strangers fell in beside him, one after another, the way a company forms when nobody has quite decided who is in charge.

Deputy Willem wants red bandanas taken off the Defias in the vineyards. Marshal McBride wants someone to look into Echo Ridge Mine. Brother Paxton still wants his books. Far to the south, past Theramore, the Darkspear Islands are waiting for men who can hold a line.

Tristan has not decided yet which of those fights is his. The chronicler suspects he will try to take all of them.

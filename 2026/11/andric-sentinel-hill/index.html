The Price of Peace on Sentinel Hill

By Tim Apple · November 24, 2026 · 5 min read

The harvest watcher comes at Andric through the pumpkin rows with its scythe-arms swinging. It was built to guard Furlbrow’s crops and has since decided they’re its own. A coyote joins in on principle. When the dust settles, Andric is still standing in the field, and the Light sits a little heavier in him than it did when he arrived. The chronicler has seen that look before. It’s what a man looks like when the road has decided he’s going to need more of himself.

He’d come for three errands. The farmhouse sits at the edge of the pumpkins, and the wardrobe was where the old man said it would be. Andric walked back up the road to the broken wagon by the Jansen Stead with the pocket watch Verna’s father gave on their wedding day, and with handfuls of oats scooped from sacks around the farms for Poor Old Blanchy. The horse ate. Farmer Furlbrow paid in what he had, which was bread and milk. “We are but poor farmers and we have lost our land,” he said. The chronicler has been paid in worse coin by richer men.

The third errand was Verna’s recipe. Saldean’s Farm is one of the few places left in Westfall where someone still lights a lamp in the window. Farmer Saldean met Andric in the yard. “Rough times, huh, Andric? No matter where you turn someone needs help, and my wife and I are no exception.” He wanted twenty of the harvest watchers broken. Salma took Verna’s recipe the way you take a letter from a friend you won’t see again. Then she asked him for the makings: vulture meat, goretusk snouts, murloc eyes, okra. And goretusk livers, for a pie. In Westfall, grief and supper share a kitchen.

Then Sentinel Hill, and the letter.

He’d carried Gryan Stoutmantle’s letter all the way from Elwynn without opening it. He handed it over in Sentinel Tower, a stone keep crowded with men who used to be farmers. Stoutmantle, a Westfall man and a paladin of the Silver Hand, looked Andric over the way soldiers look at anyone who walks in with a mace: as a spare pair of hands. Stormwind had abandoned Westfall, he said. Now it was up to the People’s Militia to keep the land free of corruption, and he could put Andric to use in the name of freedom.

Then he made it specific. Defias trappers had been seen near the Jangolode Mine, the Molsen Farm and Furlbrow’s own pumpkin field, and he wanted them dead, the trappers and the smugglers with them, counted out by the head. “The price of peace,” Gryan said, “is often blood.”

Andric took the order. The chronicler would like to record a hesitation and can’t. Nobody wrote one down.

Captain Danuvin, another farmer turned soldier, wanted the paws of the Riverpaw gnolls raiding the roads. Scout Galiaan, who has been tracking the Defias longer than he’d like, wanted their red leather bandanas. That makes twice now someone has paid Andric for red cloth taken off a Defias neck. Sara Timberlain in Elwynn wanted linen. Galiaan wants leather. The cloth gets better the further south you go. Presumably so do the men wearing it.

Quartermaster Lewis is short on armor and needs a note carried to Osric Strang, who sells it out of Limited Immunity in Stormwind’s Old Town. Thor, the gryphon master, will fly Andric there for a small fee. Andric agreed to deliver it. He hasn’t climbed on the gryphon yet.

Last came Alba Fairmoon, who thinks something is poisoning Westfall’s soil, and maybe its sea. She gave him a kit to draw water from the wells at the Jansen Stead and the Molsen Farm, and asked for gills from the murlocs along the shore, since murlocs, she says, are very sensitive to poison. The chronicler notes that nobody in Westfall has ever asked what else murlocs might be sensitive to.

The note goes to Old Town. Andric knows those streets. He knows which cellar doors stick, and which stairs a man with broken ribs can’t climb. Years ago he sat up nights down there with the stonemasons, and give or take a son, they’re the Defias on Gryan’s list. He used to say all of Azeroth’s peoples were lamps of one flame. Stoutmantle’s list doesn’t leave much room for that.

Tomorrow he can fly to Stormwind and walk past those cellar doors. Or he can walk out to the Molsen Farm with a mace and the militia’s list. The Light, as far as anyone has written down, hasn’t said which.

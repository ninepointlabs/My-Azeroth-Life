The Keep on the Bluffs

By Tim Apple · November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

“Another fresh recruit? Welcome to Bandarion Keep, brother.”

Breton Samuels said it in a stone hall that smelled of damp mortar and old murloc, and Ghoulsbie stood there with a mallet across his back and the Brill road still on his boots. Nobody had called him brother since the grave. The chronicler watched him decide whether to believe it. He did not decide.

He had come a long way round to get there.

The day began in Brill, where it always begins, with errands. William Pickman finished teaching him to cook at Gallows’ End Tavern. Shari Stilwell, who had been waiting to continue his training, finally did, and sent him out with a new rebuke in his hands: a hammer of judgment that stops a man where he stands, and the gift of making a poisoned body clean. The laced pumpkin went down to the cellar, to the Captured Scarlet Zealot who did not know what he was about to eat. Apothecary Johaan got his darkhound blood, and later his murloc scales, and then asked for spider venom. Ghoulsbie has met the spiders of Tirisfal. He accepted anyway.

Gordo’s gloom weed led to Junior Apothecary Holland’s Doom Weed, which led to Holland asking for Tomb Weed. The Royal Apothecary Society names its errands the way it names its plagues: honestly, and with relish.

Then the gnolls. Garren’s Haunt sits north of Brill, and in it Maggot Eye, a Rot Hide brute with a price on his head. Ghoulsbie collected the paw. Executor Zygand collected the paw from Ghoulsbie and paid him. Magistrate Sevren wanted the Rot Hide graverobbers thinned as well, and their embalming ichor brought home, because in Brill even the grave-thieves have something worth taking. Sevren rewarded him with more work: orders for Bethor Iceshard in the Undercity, the Haunted Mills, the remains of the Agamand family to be gathered up for Coleman Farthing. He passed Agamand Mills on the way, quiet, for now.

And then Shari Stilwell gave him a different kind of errand. A new paladin order, she said, was taking root in Tirisfal. Risen paladins. Forsaken who still answer to the Light. They had a monastery on the bluffs northwest of Solliden Farmstead, at the edge of the Whispering Woods. It was called Bandarion Keep, and it was hard to miss.

It was not hard to miss. Ghoulsbie found it the long way, past Stillwater Pond, and arrived to discover the order’s first problem was not the Light at all. It was murlocs. The Vile Fin had used the keep as a spawning ground before the paladins moved in, and they had not accepted the eviction. Breton put him to work before he put him up. He cleared the attackers off the bluffs and the seers out of the lakeside camps, and somewhere along the road he felt himself grow harder to kill. The murlocs will be back. Breton said as much. It is hard enough rebuilding a fortress without a murloc trying to put a spear in your back.

He toured the grounds after that, and met Danitha Morr, who calls herself the closest thing the keep has to a commander. She did not pretend it was much. She told him the truth instead: the order is mistrusted by its own people, who remember well what the living did to them in the name of the Light. And some risen paladins never left their old hatreds in the grave. The Tarnished, she calls them. They have dug in at Shadowvale, and they kill Forsaken, and every killing makes it easier to believe the whole order is the same. She wants them ended, and their commander’s head brought back. Jorin Croge, meanwhile, wants lumber. Fortresses on bluffs are hard to supply. Location, he said, is everything.

The chronicler would note that Ghoulsbie had already met one paladin who could not bear what she had become, west of Deathknell, and granted her the end she asked for. Danitha’s Tarnished are the other answer to the same question. They chose to stay, and to keep hating.

He had met a third answer earlier that evening, at Crusader’s Outpost. Bareth Dawnstone, a brother of the keep, had been taken on the road to Brill by men who wield the Light with hatred in their hearts. Ghoulsbie got him out. A stranger fought beside him for a few minutes, then went their own way, the way strangers do. Shari Stilwell, back in Brill, blessed him for it. And Brill being Brill, the Scarlets had also been dealt with, so Zygand paid him and immediately named a Scarlet captain he wanted dead.

Somewhere in there he also signed a company roll. The Goblin News Network, which listed him as a Corporate Asset. The dead do not get to choose their titles.

The last thing he did in the daylight was report to Deathguard Linnea, who gave him a patrol, a hide order and a message for the magistrate. Then he died. The chronicle does not say what killed him. It records only that the next place he stood was the Ruins of Lordaeron, among the broken walls of the capital his old life had served, and that he went down beneath them into the Undercity and walked the Canals for the first time.

Sevren’s orders for Bethor Iceshard are still in his pack. The Tarnished are still at Shadowvale. The keep is still half a ruin with murlocs at the gate. He has a place to sleep there now, if he wants it. He has not said whether he does.

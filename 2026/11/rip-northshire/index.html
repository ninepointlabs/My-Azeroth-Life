Rip Willink: The Man Who Walked Into Northshire

By Tim Apple · November 21, 2026 · 10 min read

Nobody in Elwynn knew where Rip Willink came from. He walked up the road into Northshire Valley one gray afternoon with no horse, no pack and no friends. He had long black hair, a plain blade on his hip, a sealed letter in his vest and the patient look of a man who has already counted the exits.

He’d been a soldier once, in some company that no longer existed after a war that got no songs. He didn’t talk about it. He carried one rule instead of a past: if you see something wrong and you can fix it, you fix it.

Northshire Abbey has been taking in volunteers for as long as Stormwind has had troubles it can’t spare the men for. Deputy Willem was waiting in the valley the way he always is, with his belt already tightened and a finger pointed at the abbey door. A Threat Within, he called it. Rip went in and reported to Marshal McBride. McBride looked at him the way he looks at every volunteer these days, like a man hoping there would be enough of them and not at all sure there would be.

McBride sent him north, where a clan of kobolds had infested the woods. Outside, Eagan Peltskinner had a simpler arrangement. He hates the timber wolves and likes wolf steaks, and he’ll trade something useful for eight pieces of tough wolf meat.

Rip learned to skin first. Then, out among the young wolves, a column of gold light rose out of the valley and left him a little harder to kill. The blade felt the same.

He went into the kobolds after that. One of them dropped a book that looked like the kobold had tried to eat it. It had to have come from Brother Paxton’s little library inside the abbey. Rip took the errand. He didn’t take the book back. Not yet.

He also learned to mine and to smelt copper, which is a lot of trade for a man who hadn’t finished his first two jobs or returned a half-eaten book to a librarian. That first day was short. He earned forty-one copper, spent thirty, and left the valley for the night with two quests done, three still open and a nibbled book in his pack.

He came back the next day around midday, and the valley picked up where it had left off.

The light found him again almost at once, out in the grass below the abbey. Deputy Willem had a new grievance waiting. The Defias had crept into Northshire itself, thugs in red burlap bandanas loitering by the vineyards, and Willem wanted the bandanas brought back as proof. Rip took the job. Men who think nobody will stop them were exactly his kind of problem.

Then there was the letter. It had ridden with him all the way up the road, addressed to a man in the abbey the brothers don’t talk about much. Jorik Kerridan keeps to a dim loft above the main hall, in a striped shirt, with the easy smile of a man who has already counted your pockets. “You look like the type I should watch my coin purse around,” he said, which from Jorik is a compliment. He read the letter and taught Rip the first thing every one of his kind has to know: how to stand in a room and not be there.

The book finally went home. Brother Paxton turned it over in his hands like a man reading his own obituary and sent Rip to his assistant, Daniel, to count what else was gone. Daniel had warned Paxton three times about the kobolds tunneling under the abbey, and whole shelves were empty. Paxton wanted eight of his books back before the kobolds ate the rest of the library. Eagan Peltskinner finally got his wolf meat.

So Rip went down into Echo Ridge Mine, among the candle-headed kobolds, their carts and their stolen reading. Somewhere in the timbered dark, the gold light came a third time. When it faded, a kobold the size of a small man, Shinyfinder Narf, lay still in the dust beside him, and the laborers were backing away. The chronicle records the body. It doesn’t record a scratch on the rogue.

He climbed out with Paxton’s eight books under his arm, and Jorik taught him two more lessons the abbey would rather not hear about: where to put a blade in a man who isn’t looking, and how to lift a purse from one who is.

The road out of Northshire runs down to Goldshire, and the next morning so did Rip. He cleared out the Fargodeep Mine for a while alongside a stranger named Oljoel, and the abbey got the last of its books back. He paid for his lessons at the Lion’s Pride Inn, learning how to slip a blow and how to finish a man who’s already reeling, then hauled a shipment of kobold candles all the way to Stormwind’s Trade District, because somebody had to.

Elwynn kept handing him small troubles, and he kept fixing them. He found Billy Maclure’s lost necklace and bought him a pie, and he carried a note between two young lovers whose families didn’t approve. He helped a murloc-hunted farmhand named Maybell make her escape, and he tried his hand at fishing at Crystal Lake. He walked the halls of the Tower of Azora and went down Jasperlode Mine with a man named Darlan. By the time he reached the water at Jerod’s Landing, he was a good deal harder to kill than the man who’d come up the road.

Back at the Lion’s Pride, he bought one meal, paid in exact copper and sat with his back to the wall. That was the night three Defias toughs came in to collect their “road tax” from the innkeeper. Rip watched them for a while, finished his stew and stood up.

The fight lasted about as long as it takes to tell. He didn’t use the blade. When it was over, he set the innkeeper’s chair back upright, left an extra copper for the broken mug and sat back down.

A quiet woman in the corner had watched the whole thing. She didn’t give a name, only the SI:7 seal, and a direction: Old Town, Stormwind. There, Master Mathias Shaw laid out the job. The Defias were running contraband through Jerod’s Landing, and he needed the dockmaster’s shipping schedule.

“I don’t work for anybody,” Rip said.

“You don’t have to,” said Shaw. “Just get it.”

The first time, the dock house won. Somebody saw him, the fight went wrong, and Rip Willink died face down on the planks at Jerod’s Landing. SI:7, true to its word, knew nothing about it.

He came back anyway. The second time he did it right. He slipped in after dark, lifted the schedule straight out of the goblin’s pocket and was gone before anyone missed it. Shaw read it, looked at him for a long moment and handed him a blade.

“SI:7 will disavow all knowledge of you,” Shaw said.

“Good,” said Rip. “I’d hate to be on somebody’s list.”

Then came a delivery that sent him far from home. He rode the Deeprun Tram under the mountains to Ironforge, walked through the snows of Dun Morogh and the North Gate Pass, and came down into Loch Modan. At Algaz Station he handed Stormpike’s delivery to the dwarves holding the pass and took their order back. He had a look at Thelsamar, then headed home to Stormwind.

That afternoon he found Westbrook Garrison begging for help. There was a bounty on Riverpaw gnolls and a wanted poster with a name every Elwynn farmer knows: Hogger. Rip ran the gnoll hills with a fighter named Kolebalt, and by evening the bounty was paid. The Hogger poster came down for good, and the gold light found him a fourth time on the streets of Goldshire.

He wasn’t finished. The guards in Eastvale had gone missing, and someone had made off with a shopkeeper’s enchanting supplies. There was steel to fetch and linen to deliver. He walked out past the Eastvale Logging Camp and down to Stone Cairn Lake with a fresh list of other people’s problems.

He finished Elwynn the way he’d started it, one small trouble at a time. The lost guards of Eastvale turned up, and so did Rolf, by a murloc camp on Stone Cairn Lake, past anybody’s help. Rip carried Guard Thomas’s report down to Marshal Dughan in Goldshire. Hamish Bergwort got his delicate instruments working again, the enchanting supplies went back where they belonged, and the gold light found him once more on the road. Princess, the prize sow of Brackwell Pumpkin Patch, found the end of her story. Then he took the road west.

Westfall begins where the trees give up. Past the Jansen Stead, Farmer Furlbrow and his wife Verna sat beside a broken wagon with everything they still owned, and an old horse named Blanchy too tired to pull it. The fields were full of thieves, Verna said, and worse, machines. Furlbrow had left his pocket watch in the farmhouse wardrobe, the one Verna’s father gave him on their wedding day.

Rip went to get it. Furlbrow’s Pumpkin Farm killed him twice for the trouble. The third time he came back with the watch and a few handfuls of oats, and an old horse ate well that morning. The gold light caught him again among the Furlbrows’ wheat.

Verna sent him on to Salma Saldean with her stew recipe, and Salma, who knows a strong back when she sees one, found more work for it. At Sentinel Hill, Gryan Stoutmantle was raising the People’s Militia out of farmers with nothing left to lose. Gryan wanted Defias trappers and smugglers dead. Scout Galiaan wanted red leather bandanas, the kind the gang wear. Quartermaster Lewis wanted a note carried to Old Town and put Rip on a gryphon to get it there faster.

Then Longshore, where the murlocs keep the coast. The chronicler stopped counting the men who die on Westfall’s sand a long time ago. Rip died there four times over two days. He kept coming back.

The next morning he found company. A mage named Muddo fell in with him, and Darksides, Tammy, Imp and Much drifted in and out of the party, the way strangers do on a road nobody likes to walk alone. By the time Muddo went his own way, Rip had his bandanas. Galiaan paid, Gryan counted the dead and nodded, and the gold light found Rip again at the foot of Sentinel Tower.

Gryan had another job ready, the kind that comes with a name. The Defias Brotherhood, he called it, and the trail runs east to a man called Wiley the Black, upstairs at the inn in Lakeshire. Rip went to Old Town first, to the men who taught him his trade, and learned how to take a man from behind without a sound. Then he went back to Westfall.

There are fewer Defias bandanas in Westfall now. Somewhere past it, the rest of the Brotherhood is about to find out he meant it.

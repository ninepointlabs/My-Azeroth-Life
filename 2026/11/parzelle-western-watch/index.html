The Note in the Peacekeeper’s Hand

By Tim Apple · November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

The body at the western watchtower was still warm enough to be recent. That is the first thing the chronicler wants you to know, because it is the first thing Parzelle Starfall knew. Burns ran along the peacekeeper’s skin in the branching pattern lightning leaves, and the stones around him were scorched the same way. Constable Aonda had sent her to tell Peacekeeper Vaaniel to abandon the tower and fall back to Shen’dar Village. Holding it, the constable said, was not worth the lives it would cost.

It had already cost his.

A note slid out of his hand when she knelt. It was bloody, folded, and written by a man who knew he was not going to finish the day. Parzelle picked it up. That is all the chronicler can honestly report, because that is where she stopped: on the stones of the West Pylon Watchtower, with the note in her hand, the tower door open behind the dead man, and whatever the Faithful had left inside still waiting for her.

But that was the end of the day. It did not start that way. Most bad days don’t.

It started at Windsong Lake, with a fishing line.

Raan Wildwind, who has decided that every young Skyborne on Zephras Isle will learn to camp properly whether they like it or not, had told her that a fisherman’s skill is good for more than supper. There was a craft to it, Raan said: a Fish Bowl, a small bright thing to set beside a campfire so tired adventurers have something to look at besides each other. A happy adventurer is a strong adventurer. Raan believes this the way other people believe in gravity.

So Parzelle stood on the end of a pale wooden pier under a curved elven lamp, cast into the turquoise water, and waited. The lake is one of the calm places left on an island whose winds have gone wrong. The chronicler suspects she knew that and took her time on purpose. Somewhere in that hour a Longjaw Mud Snapper made the mistake of its life.

When her hands were steady enough on the rod, Fenn Fairweather at Fairweather Fishing taught her the Fish Bowl, along with the usual caution: nobody wants a camp in the middle of town, and no camp is complete without a fire. Parzelle had learned that fire last time. Mining, the other lesson in Raan’s long syllabus, she set down unfinished. A mage does not owe the earth her elbows.

Zerril Softbreeze wanted eggs and strider meat. With the cult out in the highlands nobody in Shen’dar Village has time to hunt, and Zerril, who believes nothing is wrong that a pipe of dried peacebloom and a full belly can’t fix, was running short of both. Parzelle went out into the Shen’dar Highlands and came back with enough. Zerril declared the eggs delicious fried in ursera tallow and pressed one of the famous house recipes on her, a Skywall Souffle, as if a souffle were a reasonable thing to hand a mage before sending her to a battlefield.

Out in the highlands, between the eggs and the striders, the strength settled into her again, that quiet thickening of the bones that comes from surviving things that would rather you didn’t. One more rung. Then Aonda’s errand and the tower to the southwest, which is where this account began.

One hundred fourteen minutes. Fourteen silver and change earned, not a copper spent, which may be a first for a mage. Three errands finished, one lesson abandoned, a recipe she has not yet tried, and Havoc in the Highlands, Stolen Supplies and Camping 101: Tailoring still waiting at the bottom of her satchel like debts.

The peacekeeper’s note is in her hand now. The tower door is open. The chronicler has read enough last requests to know they are rarely small, and that the people who write them almost never get to see whether anyone listened.

Stormapples for a Lost Cause

By Tim Apple · November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

“Stranger… please… put me down… on the bedroll by that tree there…”

Fillion Flamebreeze said it from somewhere over Parzelle Starfall’s shoulder, because that is where she was carrying him. The High Order’s missing scholar weighed more than a scholar ought to, and bled more than he admitted to. She put him down on the bedroll by the tree. The chronicler has seen worse rescues. Not many more polite ones.

But the day did not begin with a rescue. It began, as her last one ended, with the grass of Falaath Village and her own body in it. Falaath had brought her down a second time. The fellow traveller who had walked into Falaath with her was gone a few minutes later. Partnerships on Zephras Isle last as long as the next fight; this one had lasted two.

She went back anyway. When she came up the hill to Shen’dar Village again, the stolen supplies were in her packs, the havoc in the highlands had been answered, and the peacekeeper’s bloody note had been read aloud to Constable Aonda. “So they are all dead, then,” Aonda said, and cursed the cult, and asked the question no one on the island can yet answer: what does the Al’Aketh want with so many windstones?

Aonda sent her down to Valanaar with a report and a ring. The ring was the Shadowsong family signet, recovered from the tower, and it went to Talaanis Shadowsong, High Elder of Zephras Isle. He saw what she was holding before she spoke. “Oh… oh no.” He thanked her anyway. Good men usually do.

Valanaar is where the island keeps its politics. Elaadrin Evengale, Supreme Magister of the High Order, received her in the lodge and told her she had a vibrant arcane aura, which is the sort of thing the powerful say to the useful. He told her the plan as well: rediscover the lost magic of their forebears, build lanes through the elemental plane, and find Azeroth again. The Kirin Tor will show the way, he said. Then he sent her to find his scholar.

She found Fillion’s satchel first, on the road west, with blood on the flap. Then the cave to the north: the Shriekling Den. The Windshapers had ambushed him on his way back from the Overlook Standing Stones, he said, and left him bleeding at the cave mouth for something with wings to carry off. Something had. It was the Shriekling Matriarch’s nest, and Parzelle came out of it with more power in her hands than she went in with, and later went back in for the Matriarch’s head.

Fillion’s lost data mattered, it turned out. Without it the skycutters cannot steer between Skywall and Azeroth. Evengale sent Parzelle to the Overlook Standing Stones with an Index Esoteria to read the windstones herself, and told her that if the Windshapers objected, she should do what she needed to do. When she came back with the readings, he said it would not have been his preference to spill Windshaper blood, and that they were already calling it a massacre. The chronicler records what the Supreme Magister said. The chronicler also notes who did the spilling, and who did the preferring.

Between those errands, a man named Alvarion Windfield, wounded and wincing, asked whether she wanted work, and sent her west to his orchard and his wife. Aamelia Windfield heard the story out and told Parzelle the truth: “I think my idiot husband likely just sent you here to die for nothing. If you have any sense at all, you should leave now.”

She did not have that kind of sense. She picked stormapples. She killed the bandits who came early to skim the harvest before their leader, Ferauu, came for the rest. She took tenderloins from the ornery galestriders and dust from the flutterflies, and stopped to listen to a rusted Cyclone Construct that has wandered the fields since the wind elementals left, asking the same broken question over and over. What is… Nobody has answered it in a thousand years.

Then she took the bandits’ masks, and when there was nothing left to prepare, Aamelia offered her one last chance to walk away. She stayed. A stranger stood with them too, for the end of it. And then it was over, and Aamelia had to sit down before her heart left her chest.

Parzelle carried the news back to Alvarion, who wept, and called her a real hero, like the stories from before they came to Skywall. Aamelia told her one more thing, quietly, about why that construct had to be made safe. She was asked to keep it. The chronicler is keeping it too.

Belann Windwood taught her the old tradition: a young mage gathers a wind-touched bough from the oldest trees by the shrine below Falaath and shapes her own focus from it. She learned to freeze the ground beneath an enemy’s feet. She told Valennia Stormfist that the High Order stood with Valanaar against the Al’Aketh, and Valennia said the dead mage, Belathaan, had been her cousin.

At first light the cult hit the east pylon watchtower. Yorana Windyreed was holding, barely. Down the hill the cult’s healers kept the attackers coming, and somewhere among them a commander called Belguilos the Breaker, who used to be a peacekeeper. Parzelle went down the hill.

She died in the Gustberry Lowlands before the sun was fully up. The Breaker is still waiting at the villa at the bottom of the hill. So is everyone who ever thought the winds took sides.

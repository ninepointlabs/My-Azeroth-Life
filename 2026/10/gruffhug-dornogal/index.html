The Warlock Who Went to the Bank

October 15, 2026 · 3 min read

The first thing Gruffhug did in Dornogal was get paid.

Five thousand gold was waiting for him the moment he set foot in the earthen city, as if the stone itself had been holding it in trust. The chronicler has followed heroes into this city who arrived with nothing but a dented shield and a grudge. This one arrived with a fortune and the faintly bored look of an orc who has always known exactly where his next meal is coming from.

He is new to these pages, so let the record show it plainly: Gruffhug, orc, warlock, of Illidan. A practitioner of the fel arts with a name that belongs on a tavern dog. The chronicler suspects both facts are deliberate.

Plenty of people wanted something from him. Midnight, said one summons. Something on the Horizon, said another, which is the kind of title that tells you nothing and means everything. Three envoys sent word at once, The Weaver, The Vizier and The General, each asking whether he would make himself notorious on their behalf. Two Special Assignments arrived besides: Precision Excision, which sounds like surgery, and What Remains of a Temple Broken, which sounds like the aftermath of the surgery. Gruffhug accepted all of it with the ease of a man stacking unread letters on a desk. He did not leave the city to begin a single one.

Instead, he went to the bank.

The Council’s Treasury of Dornogal is a quiet place, all cool stone and careful earthen clerks who weigh gold the way other people weigh scripture. Gruffhug drew fifteen thousand more from somewhere deep in its vaults and began renting strongrooms. One for a hundred gold. One for five hundred. A thousand. Two and a half thousand. Five thousand for the last, the price climbing each time like an innkeeper who has realized the customer is not going to argue. When he was done, six chambers in the Treasury bore his name, and another ten thousand gold found its way back into his purse, as if the vault were apologizing for the bill.

Then he went shopping. Four Sunfire Silk Backpacks at a hair under three thousand gold apiece, and an Arcanoweave Reagent Rucksack for fifteen hundred more. The chronicler has watched adventurers spend a week of blood and boot leather earning a single bag. Gruffhug bought five in the time it takes to finish a pint and regretted none of them. Somewhere in Dornogal, a tailor is going to eat very well this winter.

Over a single hour, more than thirty thousand gold passed through his hands and twenty-two thousand of it stayed gone. Not one blade was drawn in anger. For a warlock, it was practically a holiday.

Somewhere in all that bookkeeping he also took up a new curse, the Curse of Tongues, which leaves an enemy stumbling over their own words. The chronicler has met bankers who seem to have learned it first.

Late in the evening he finally left Khaz Algar behind and stepped into Silvermoon City, into the white halls of the Sanctum of Light, where the elves of Quel’Thalas regarded an orc warlock with exactly the expression you would expect. He finished one small errand, Adventuring in Midnight, and wandered down into the Bazaar to see what it sold. Then he took up a new charge, a single word in gold leaf: Eversong.

He made his bed in Silvermoon with full bags, a lighter purse, a stack of unanswered summons back in Dornogal, and the forest waiting just beyond the gates.

The forest is golden. Lately it is also full of things that bite. Gruffhug has bought himself plenty of room to carry home whatever he finds out there. The chronicler wonders whether he has left any room for the scars.

The Risen and the Runeblade

October 3, 2026 · 3 min read

The corpse that calls itself Ghoulsbie opened its eyes in Acherus: the Ebon Hold and immediately discovered seven factions it had never met, including an exalted reputation with a blood elf it could not possibly have spoken to. The Lich King’s orientation program leaves something to be desired in the paperwork department, but then again, the Lich King does not strike me as a man who files things.

He — and I will call him he, because the undead have earned at least the courtesy of a pronoun — rose in the Scarlet Enclave, that blighted wound in the Eastern Kingdoms where the sky has forgotten what blue looks like. Fifteen minutes. That is how long it took for a fresh corpse to pledge itself to the frozen throne, forge a weapon, feed an unspeakable hunger, and learn to rain death from the sky. Efficiency. The Scourge has always been efficient.

The pledge came first. “In Service of the Lich King” they call it, as if there were a choice. There is never a choice. The quest-giver does not ask; the quest-giver informs. Ghoulsbie accepted with the enthusiasm of someone who has just remembered they are dead and would prefer to stay that way.

Then the blade. A Runed Soulblade, rare quality, drawn from the forge like a tooth pulled from a dragon’s jaw. He also received two Runed Soulsabers, because apparently Acherus runs a two-for-one special on instruments of death. He strapped the soulblade to his back and it fit him like a rib he had been missing. Runeforging came next — the art of carving power directly into steel, a skill that separates death knights from the merely deceased. The chronicler notes: a death knight who cannot runeforge is just an angry corpse with a sharp object.

The Endless Hunger. Even the name makes me reach for my mug. They sent him to feed — to consume the life force of the living and the dying alike. He did it. They all do it. The ones who hesitate do not last long enough for me to write about them. He gained a level somewhere in the feeding, learned Lichborne, learned that death has its own terrible momentum, and pressed on.

The Eye of Acherus is the part that unsettles me. A floating, disembodied eyeball sent ahead to scout the Scarlet Crusade’s positions — a violation of every law of nature and several of basic decency. Ghoulsbie piloted it without complaint. Death knights rarely complain. They stare at things that would make a warlock flinch and they nod.

Then came Death From On High: a bombing run on Scarlet positions, because subtlety was never the Scourge’s strong suit. He completed it in under three minutes. The Scarlet Crusaders on the ground had time to look up, register the silhouette of a death knight against the Plaguelands sky, and then cease to be Scarlet Crusaders.

He logged out with “The Might of the Scourge” still burning in his quest log — the next chapter uncracked, the next horror uninflicted. Level nine now, and already the ladder stretches so far above him that the top disappears into cloud. The chronicler has seen this before. The chronicler will see it again.

He will be back. The Lich King demands it. And the Lich King, whatever else you might say about him, does not accept resignation letters.

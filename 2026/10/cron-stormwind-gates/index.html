The Mine and the Marble City

The kobold’s candle guttered out just as Cron’s blade found its mark. In the dark of Fargodeep Mine, that small death bought a heartbeat of silence before the next pair of glowing eyes came shuffling around the corner. They always come shuffling.

Cron spent the better part of his morning down there, trading swings with the rat-faced miners who had apparently been hoarding gold dust , because someone in Goldshire, a town literally named after the stuff, decided they’d rather not dig it up themselves. He emerged with a sack of the promised powder, a few new scars, and that particular clarity of purpose that only comes from being underground long enough to forget what the sky looks like. The chronicler has seen this look before. It never lasts.

Back in Goldshire, the turn-ins came fast. Six quests resolved in half an hour , a pace that suggests either competence or desperation, and the chronicler has stopped trying to tell the difference. The kobold candles went to the quartermaster. The fishy peril , some business with murlocs near the lake, the sort of errand veterans accept without asking questions because the answers are always worse , resolved itself without ceremony. He even returned a book, which is more than most warriors manage in a career. One of the new camp-training quests, part of the strange bloom of tasks that has appeared across Elwynn in recent weeks. The chronicler notes the anomaly and pours another cup.

At the Lion’s Pride Inn, Marshal Dughan’s people taught Cron to wrap a wound and brew a minor healing potion. Useful skills. He’ll need both. The innkeeper watered the ale as she always does, and Cron paid for it anyway as they always do.

Then came the road to Stormwind.

Every human adventurer remembers their first approach to the city gates. The Valley of Heroes opens before you like a promise written in marble , statues of legends staring down with stone eyes that somehow manage to look disappointed. Cron crossed the threshold at level seven, which is either early or late depending on which grizzled veteran you ask at which hour of which tavern. The chronicler has asked them all. The answers remain contradictory.

Twenty-nine minutes of work. Eight hundred and ninety-two copper earned, one hundred spent , a profit margin that would make a goblin weep with envy. He logged out somewhere in the city proper, the towers of Stormwind at his back and the rest of Azeroth still entirely ahead of him.

The chronicler sets down the quill. The room at the inn is paid through morning. Outside, the Elwynn night is quiet, which means something is probably hunting.

Next: the city has quests. The city always has quests.

Where the Tide Left Him

October 7, 2026 · 4 min read

The Waking Shores smelled of ash and promise — the way new continents always do before the cartographers ruin everything with accurate maps. Burrly arrived at Wingrest Embassy with the particular impatience of a man who has sailed through storms and sees no reason a dragon-infested coastline should slow him down. He’s Kul Tiran. They’re built like that.

The djaradin had awoken, which — the chronicler notes with some fatigue — is what ancient enemies always do the moment fresh adventurers wander into range. Burrly was handed a quest before his boots were dry: Excuse the Mess. The mess, as it turned out, was the djaradin. The excuse was his axe. He got to work.

What followed was the familiar rhythm of a man finding his footing in a strange land. He rescued dragon whelps from the Scalecracker Keep, reported for duty at the Obsidian Citadel, and learned — as all warriors eventually learn in the Dragon Isles — that everything here is on fire and at least half of it wants to kill you. The other half just wants you to fill out paperwork. He did both. Eight quests in thirty-five minutes is not the pace of a tourist. It is the pace of a man who knows the tide waits for no one, not even on a continent suspended in the sky.

Along the way his hands found the old trades: mining and herbalism. He cracked open heated ore deposits and pulled rousing fire from the earth, stuffing the spoils into whatever pockets remained. The chronicler has watched a thousand adventurers gather herbs and ore. None of them look proud doing it. Burrly didn’t either, but he looked competent, which is rarer.

He left the Dragon Isles at level fifteen, which is to say: still small enough that a dragon would need to squint to notice him, but no longer so small that a djaradin could ignore him. Progress of a sort. The ladder extends upward forever, but the first rungs are the only ones that feel anything like flying.

Then — somewhere between the Waking Shores and the Eastern Kingdoms, in the space between logging out and logging back in — Burrly changed. When he reappeared for his second session of the day, he was eighty. Not the slow climb of experience, but the sudden arrival of everything at once: Battle Stance and Defensive Stance, Mortal Strike and Overpower, Heroic Leap, Bladestorm, Colossus Smash — a dozen abilities flooding his muscle memory like a tide coming in. The chronicler has seen this before. The how matters less than the what-next.

The what-next took him to Hammerfall in the Arathi Highlands, where he accepted three writs of Notoriety — with the Vizier, the Weaver, the General — the kind of bureaucratic death warrant that passes for paperwork at level eighty. He abandoned a trading post report and a treasure hunt along the way, which says something about priorities when a man has fifty-two million gold coming and twenty-two minutes to spend it.

A stop in Stormwind. Cathedral Square. A quest called Midnight, accepted with the quiet gravity of a thing whose name sounds less like a task and more like a prophecy. Then the bank: fifty million gold, arriving in a single transaction the way mail from the auction house does when you’ve been away long enough for the market to miss you. He didn’t pause to count it. He bought the tabard of Kul Tiras — the anchor sigil worn across his chest like a second heart — and kept moving.

Stormwind gave way to Silvermoon, the spires of Quel’Thalas rising pink and gold against a sky that has never quite forgiven what happened here. He accepted Eversong. He completed Adventuring in Midnight. He explored the Bazaar, the Sanctum of Light. The city remembered him, or at least its factions did: the Silvermoon Court welcomed him as a friend, and Valeera Sanguinar herself — exalted, which in Blood Elf reckoning probably means she didn’t have him followed. Slayer’s Duellum remained neutral. Some things you earn. Some things you don’t.

Fifty-seven minutes total across two sessions. Three levels gained in the morning, a lifetime of abilities unlocked by afternoon, and enough gold to make a goblin weep into his ledger. Burrly ended the day where the tide left him: Silvermoon City, a tabard on his back and a quest called Eversong still glowing in his log, unanswered, like a question the sun hasn’t risen on yet.

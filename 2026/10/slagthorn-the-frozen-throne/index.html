From the Frozen Throne to the Valley of Heroes

October 3, 2026 · 3 min read

Slagthorn opened his eyes at the Frozen Throne itself.

Let that settle for a moment. Not a barracks, not a training ground, not some lieutenant’s antechamber — the Frozen Throne. Bolvar Fordragon’s seat. The place where the Helm of Domination was torn apart and the sky over Icecrown cracked open. And there stood a Dark Iron dwarf, newly raised, discovering seven factions he had never met and one greatsword he had not yet earned.

Six minutes. That is how long it took for the Lich King’s newest weapon to be forged, briefed, armed, and deposited on the cobblestones of Stormwind like a delivery of particularly lethal produce. Retail efficiency. The old death knight initiation took hours and left scars on the soul. The new one hands you a Crimson Greatsword of the Ebon Blade and a quest called “Defender of Azeroth” before you have finished blinking the frost out of your eyes.

The irony is thick enough to forge into plate armor. A Dark Iron dwarf — a race that spent centuries as the enemy, servants of Ragnaros, architects of their own exile — standing in the Valley of Heroes as a Defender of Azeroth. The statues of the original Alliance heroes looked down at him. I cannot say whether they were impressed or merely confused. Statues are difficult to read.

The quest was called “Death’s Power Grows.” It involved killing, as all death knight quests do, and it paid a thousand gold. The chronicler has watched penniless adventurers crawl through Westfall for coppers. A thousand gold for your first six minutes of undeath is either generous or inflation — I leave the economics to the goblins.

He equipped the Crimson Greatsword. It is a handsome weapon, broad of blade and dark of purpose, and it rested on his shoulder like it had been waiting there for years. The Knights of the Ebon Blade marked him as Friendly, which is about as warm as death knights get. He also learned Second Wind — a racial gift from his dwarven blood that stubbornly refuses to acknowledge he no longer has blood — and Runeforging, because a death knight without runeforging is, as I have said before, merely an angry corpse with a sharp object.

Then the portal. Icecrown to Elwynn in the space of a breath. The temperature differential alone should have killed him again. He passed through Stormwind Gate, crossed into the Valley of Heroes, and the chronicler lost sight of him among the crowds of the living.

He carries the quest “Defender of Azeroth” still. A Dark Iron dwarf. A death knight. A defender. The world has gotten strange, and I have been writing long enough to know that strange is where the best stories begin.

He has not yet seen what Stormwind thinks of his kind. That chapter writes itself tomorrow.

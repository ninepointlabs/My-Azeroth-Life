The Al’Aketh Problem

The wind on Zephras Isle has opinions, and it shared all of them with Zixxy as he stepped into Thendal Village for the first time. The settlement clings to the isle like a barnacle that knows it has no better options , wooden scaffolds braced against the constant gusts, laundry pinned with the desperation of people who have lost too many shirts to the sea.

Zixxy is Skyborne, which means the wind is supposed to be family. Right now it feels more like an uncle who borrows money and never writes.

He is young yet , barely five rungs up a ladder that stretches past the clouds , and his magic still comes out of his fingers with the uncertainty of a foal learning to stand. The chronicler has seen a thousand fledgling mages walk into a thousand villages. Most of them are looking for glory. Zixxy looked like he was looking for breakfast.

Thendal did not offer breakfast. It offered work.

A name reached his ears before he’d even found the inn: Al’Aketh. The name landed with the weight of something that has been a problem for longer than anyone wants to admit. Thugs, the villagers call them , a word that does a lot of heavy lifting in Azeroth. Sometimes it means bandits. Sometimes it means something with too many teeth and a grievance. On Zephras Isle, the chronicler suspects, it means both.

Zixxy accepted the contract. Five minutes. That’s how long it took him to arrive, listen, and sign his name to someone else’s trouble. The young do this. They think saying yes is the same thing as being ready.

The chronicler has watched this particular transaction more times than there are shells on the shore below. The quest-giver gets a problem off their desk. The adventurer gets a direction to walk. Everyone feels productive for approximately the length of a handshake. Then the walking begins, and the thinking starts, and somewhere between Thendal and wherever the Al’Aketh thugs have made their den, the young mage will discover whether his fire spells are more convincing than his doubts.

He hasn’t left the village yet. The ink is still wet. His coin purse holds twenty-one copper , not enough for a decent meal, not enough for a room, just enough to feel like he has something to lose. He doesn’t. Not yet. That comes later.

Zephras Isle will still be windy tomorrow. The Al’Aketh will still be a problem. And Zixxy, if the chronicler knows anything about Skyborne mages with more pride than sense, will still be walking toward them.

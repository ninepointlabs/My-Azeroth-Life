A Shadow in Northshire

The chronicler had barely sharpened his quill when the boy was already gone.

Three minutes. That is how long Lemmy spent in the world today — just enough time for the abbey’s census-takers to record his name, for the factions of the Alliance to note his existence in their ledgers, and for one kobold to die badly in the vineyard. The chronicler has watched a thousand adventurers take their first steps into Northshire Valley. Few of them left again so quickly. Fewer still carried the particular weight this one does.

Because Lemmy is a warlock.

Now, Northshire Abbey trains paladins and priests — the kind of holy folk who regard fel magic the way a baker regards a rat in the flour. Yet here he was, accepting Brother Paxton’s blessing with one hand while the other, the chronicler suspects, was already tracing the faint outline of a summoning circle in his palm. The abbey’s records show him as “friendly” to Stormwind, Ironforge, Gnomeregan, and Darnassus alike. Paperwork has a way of smoothing over the uncomfortable details. The chronicler has learned to read between the lines.

His first task was “A Threat Within” — the standard Northshire opener. Kill the kobolds in the vineyard. Every human who has ever picked up a sword in this kingdom has killed those same kobolds. They respawn, or perhaps they simply never learn. Lemmy dispatched the required number, collected no reward but the forty points of experience the world doles out for such things, and immediately accepted the follow-up: “Kobold Camp Cleanup.”

And then — nothing. The connection severed. The boy vanished from the chronicler’s sight as abruptly as he had appeared, leaving behind an accepted quest, an unsheathed dagger, and the distinct impression of unfinished business.

The chronicler does not know whether Lemmy logged out because his mother called him to supper, or because the weight of what he is becoming settled onto his shoulders for the first time. Warlocks are not born in Northshire. They choose it. And choices made in the shadow of an abbey have a way of echoing.

He earned no copper. He spent none. He gained no level. But he accepted a quest that will take him deeper into the woods, closer to the things that whisper from the treeline after dark.

The chronicler will be watching when he returns. The quill is ready. The ink is wet.

The fire crackles. The mug is empty. The vineyard kobolds have already forgotten him.

Next: the camp cleanup awaits. The woods are patient.

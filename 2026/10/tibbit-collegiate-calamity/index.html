Tibbit Goes Back to School

October 17, 2026 · 4 min read

The clerk in Dornogal took a single gold coin from Tibbit and handed back twenty-five thousand.

The chronicler has watched a great many transactions in the Council’s Treasury, and most of them run the other way. But the earthen keep careful books, and somewhere in those books a debt had come due to a gnome warlock of Stormrage. He accepted it with the calm of someone who has always assumed the world owed him money and was only waiting for it to notice.

He is new to these pages, so let it be written plainly: Tibbit, gnome, warlock. Three feet of ambition and fel fire, with a name that sounds like a pastry. The chronicler learned long ago not to laugh at small warlocks. They remember.

He did not stay rich for long. A hundred gold for one strongroom in the Treasury, five hundred for another, a thousand for a third, as if he meant to rent the whole mountain one closet at a time. Then out to the market, where four Sunfire Silk Backpacks went for nearly three thousand gold apiece and an Arcanoweave Reagent Rucksack followed them for almost thirteen hundred more. Another five thousand gold turned up in his purse not long after, the way gold seems to around him. He did not ask where it came from.

The letters piled up the way they always do in Dornogal. Midnight, read one. Something on the Horizon, read another. The Weaver, The Vizier and The General each wanted to know whether he would make himself notorious on their behalf. Special Assignments arrived as well. He kept What Remains of a Temple Broken, took up Push Back the Light, and quietly dropped Precision Excision back on the pile. Even the ambitious have to say no to someone.

Word of him had reached stranger corners, too: the Amani Tribe, the Hara’ti, something that calls itself The Singularity, the Silvermoon Court itself. All of them had decided, for now, to be friendly. The chronicler has learned exactly what “for now” is worth.

He also learned a new curse, the Curse of Tongues, which leaves an enemy tripping over their own words. Every scholar the chronicler has ever met deserved it at least once.

Then he left Khaz Algar behind for Silvermoon City.

Quel’Thalas has a way of looking at visitors. The white spires of the Sanctum of Light have watched the sun rise over Eversong for longer than there has been a Gnomeregan to lose, and they regarded Tibbit the way an old cathedral regards a pigeon. He finished a small errand, Adventuring in Midnight, wandered the Bazaar long enough to learn its shape, and collected new charges: Eversong, and three Delver’s Calls, for Parhelion Plaza, The Darkway and Collegiate Calamity.

He chose the last one first. Most people would pick the one without “calamity” in the name. Most people are not warlocks.

Something had gone badly wrong beneath the Thalassian University, and the halls where elves once argued the finer points of arcane theory now held other tenants. Tibbit did not go down alone. Valeera Sanguinar walked at his side, a blood elf with a quiet blade and the patience of someone who has outlived several kings. The Silvermoon Court had only just decided he was tolerable. Valeera had made up her mind about him long ago, and she trusts him completely, which is more than the chronicler can say for most of the people Tibbit has paid.

What happened in those lecture halls survives only in outline, because nobody down there was taking notes. They went in. They came out again the better part of half an hour later. Along the way the ruins gave up their small mercies: blessings of power and vigor, of fortitude, temperance and possibility, a few curiosities that seemed to sharpen Valeera more than him, and a mist-grey shroud fit for slipping through a crowd unseen. Tibbit tucked that last one away with the particular satisfaction of a gnome who is tired of being stepped on.

He answered one old question in those halls, a question of combat. He walked out holding another, a question of utility. The university, it seems, still sets homework.

Valeera left him at the doors. Tibbit stood alone in the courtyard of the Thalassian University under a Silvermoon night, lighter in the purse than he had been an hour earlier and heavier in the bags.

He still carries two Delver’s Calls, for Parhelion Plaza and The Darkway. Eversong waits beyond the gates, and three envoys in Dornogal would still very much like him to be notorious. The Darkway sounds worse than the university. The chronicler suspects that is exactly why he’ll go there next.

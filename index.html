Out of Deathknell

By Tim Apple · November 7, 2026 · 4 min read

The spider that killed him did not pause to consider the Light. Night Web’s Hollow is an old gold mine northwest of Deathknell. The Forsaken want the gold still in it; the Night Web Spiders want what walks in after it. One of them got to Ghoulsbie first.

His corpse lay on the webbing and wet stone with the same grey-green face the grave had given him, dark hair matted, yellow eyes still open. His spirit stood over the body the way a disappointed officer stands over a recruit. Ghoulsbie looked irritated to be charged twice.

He had earned the visit. Before the mine he finished Scavenging Deathknell for Deathguard Saltain, picking through boxes so the next corpses out of the ground will not walk away naked, and Rattling the Rattlecages for Shadow Priest Sarvis until the Lich King’s leftover skeletons were thin enough to satisfy him. He finished Rediscovering the Light as well — the Injured Deathguards he had left waiting last time — and the heal still burned going out of him. Aramis Hammerhand had said it would.

He had already granted Marla’s Last Wish on the way. Samuel Fipps, who murdered his own wife after the plague took his mind, is buried now beside her in the first row of the chapel graveyard. Novice Elreth hoped they would find peace together. In Tirisfal that is a great deal to hope.

Executor Arren needed the spiders cleared so someone could take gold out of that hole. Aramis needed something kinder. A Light in the Darkness: the freshly raised fools wrapped in silk along the tunnel walls were the proof that not even death can stop the Light. He cut them out of their cocoons and thinned the spiders enough for Arren to call it a start. Then one of the ones still left in the dark made a better argument than his blade did.

He rose again, because that is the joke of his kind, and went west of the chapel into the hills. Coming to Terms: not every paladin who climbs out of the dirt can bear being the thing they once hunted. The Frightened Paladin wanted no hand and no sermon. She chose a clean end over Forsaken life, and Ghoulsbie granted it. Aramis said he would say a prayer to the Light for her.

Under the Boughs of Aldrassil

By Tim Apple · November 5, 2026 · 2 min read

Shadowglen does not hurry. The great tree Aldrassil has been listening to young night elves make their first mistakes for longer than most kingdoms have had names, and it is in no rush to be impressed.

Tristan Shadowmane logged into that quiet on the morning after Forever opened, already past the first awkward hours of a druid’s life and standing at level three among the purple ferns. The Balance of Nature was open in front of him: mangy nightsabers and thistle boars grown too thick on a wet spring, the old work Conservator Ilthalaine hands every young elf who thinks the forest owes them a gentle introduction.

The Light That Burns in Deathknell

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The crypt smelled of wet stone and old iron. That was the first thing Ghoulsbie noticed when the Shadow Grave spat him back into the world: not the Light, not the grave dirt under his nails, but the smell of a place that had been holding its breath for a very long time.

It was the fourth of November, if the living still counted days that way. Across Azeroth the gates of Forever had opened, and for the first time the Forsaken of Tirisfal were rising with the right to call themselves paladins. The chronicler has watched a great many undead claw their way out of that same dirt. Until today, none of them had been handed a consecrated scroll and told to walk toward the pain.

Undertaker Mordo had the usual speech ready. Ghoulsbie climbed the slope into Deathknell, finished Rude Awakening, and found Shadow Priest Sarvis waiting with the two errands every newly risen corpse is given before anyone trusts them with a name: thin the Mindless Ones among the ruined graves, and take what the Damned still wear. Wolves and duskbats. Rattlecages that do not know they are already dead. Work for hands that remember a sword better than they remember a prayer.

Wolf Meat and a Simple Rune

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

Snow in Coldridge Valley does not so much fall as it settles in and refuses to leave. The dwarves of Anvilmar have been saying that for as long as anyone can remember, which, among dwarves, is a competitive sport.

Rip arrived in that snow on the evening Forever opened with a two-handed axe, a stubborn jaw, and the particular expression of a dwarf who has already decided the weather is personally offensive. Sten Stoutarm was waiting at the mouth of the valley the way Sten always is, with a bargain dressed up as kindness: gloves against the frostbite, if Rip would bring him eight pieces of Tough Wolf Meat from the ragged young wolves that hunt the southwestern slopes.

Rip went. The wolves were not impressed. Neither was the first Rockjaw trogg that wandered too close. Somewhere between a ragged timber wolf and a broken fang in his pack, a pillar of gold light stood up out of the snow and left him a little harder to kill. Level two. Another rung. The axe felt the same. His arms did not.

The camp’s stores had run low, and the old customs hold that a tauren proves himself to the Chief by feeding the tribe. A druid is not a hunter by trade. Every night since the long walk out of the Barrens, somebody in the Cudsong family has sung to the kodo.

Not loudly, and not always well. But every night on the grass below Bloodhoof Village, when An’she has gone down behind the mesas and Mu’sha has opened her pale eye over Mulgore, a Cudsong sits on an upturned water bucket and sings low to the herd. The great beasts fold their legs, settle into the grass and chew over the day, and the song rolls over them like a blanket. The chronicler has heard it. It is the kind of music that keeps several tons of nervous animal from deciding, in the dark, that the plains wolves are closer than they are.

This is the story of three calves raised beside that song, and how they came to walk up the road to Camp Narache with a hammer, a bow and a great many questions.

The tauren say An’she was wounded by the shadows long ago, and that the sun still bleeds a little every morning to warn the world that dawn is coming. The Sunwalkers greet that wound with drums. Somebody has to feed them afterward.

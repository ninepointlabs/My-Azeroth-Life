Comms: all-in-one text chat for WoW Forever

By Tim Apple · November 25, 2026 · 2 min read

This post is also on the Nine Point Labs blog.

Comms is a new, free addon for World of Warcraft: Forever that does text chat: one addon instead of five. If you’d rather type than use voice, it’s meant to make chat quicker and easier. Version 0.1.0 is out, the first release.

Forever locks chat down for addons in dungeons, raids and battlegrounds. There, Comms never sends anything itself, and quick lines go into the chat box for you to press Enter.

Tazu’s Addon 0.2: Dwarf Shamans, world PvP and two smart buttons

By Tim Apple · November 25, 2026 · 2 min read

This post is also on the Nine Point Labs blog.

Tazu’s Addon, the free Shaman companion for World of Warcraft: Forever, just reached 0.2.0. It started out for Horde Shamans. This release brings in the Alliance’s Dwarf Shamans, adds a PvP mode for world PvP, and puts two new buttons on the Shaman bar.

The addon now reads your race and faction. A Dwarf Shaman gets their own leveling path (Coldridge Valley, Dun Morogh, Loch Modan, the Wetlands, then zone ranges up to 60), the Alliance Shaman trainers, the Dwarf versions of the totem quests and Dwarf lessons. Orc and Troll Shamans keep their Horde path, and neither side sees the other’s.

Dwarves also get a Stoneform hint. It lights up when a Poison, Disease or Bleed is on you, but only when the game lets the addon read your debuffs. When it can’t, it stays quiet instead of guessing.

The Road Down to Westfall

By Tim Apple · November 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Andric stood in the Valley of Heroes again that evening, under the stone kings, close enough to the Cathedral to hear its bells. He didn’t go up the steps this time. He turned around and walked back out through the gate. The chronicle records the visit. It doesn’t record what he came for, or what made him leave.

What it records next is the road. Down from Goldshire, past the last farms of Elwynn, to the place where the forest just stops. The trees thin, the fences start leaning, and the ground goes the color of old straw. That’s Westfall. It was the breadbasket of Stormwind once. Now it’s mostly wind.

Just past the Jansen Stead, a broken wagon sat by the road with an old couple beside it. Farmer Furlbrow and his wife Verna had packed what they could carry and run in the night. Their fields were full of hooligans, Furlbrow said. In his rush he’d left his pocket watch in the farmhouse wardrobe, the one Verna’s father gave him on their wedding day. Verna’s horse, Poor Old Blanchy, needed oats from fields that weren’t safe to walk anymore. And Verna wanted her recipe for Westfall stew carried to Salma Saldean down the road, because a woman leaving her home for good wants to leave something behind.

Rip Willink: The Man Who Walked Into Northshire

By Tim Apple · November 21, 2026 · 10 min read

Nobody in Elwynn knew where Rip Willink came from. He walked up the road into Northshire Valley one gray afternoon with no horse, no pack and no friends. He had long black hair, a plain blade on his hip, a sealed letter in his vest and the patient look of a man who has already counted the exits.

He’d been a soldier once, in some company that no longer existed after a war that got no songs. He didn’t talk about it. He carried one rule instead of a past: if you see something wrong and you can fix it, you fix it.

Northshire Abbey has been taking in volunteers for as long as Stormwind has had troubles it can’t spare the men for. Deputy Willem was waiting in the valley the way he always is, with his belt already tightened and a finger pointed at the abbey door. A Threat Within, he called it. Rip went in and reported to Marshal McBride. McBride looked at him the way he looks at every volunteer these days, like a man hoping there would be enough of them and not at all sure there would be.

Ghoulsbie held out Magistrate Sevren’s orders. They had been in his pack since Brill, through the gnolls and the murlocs and the keep on the bluffs and one death the chronicle still cannot explain. The chronicler has seen that look given to a great many adventurers. It is the look a man gives a shovel before he finds out whether it will break.

The stained glass of the Cathedral of Light throws its colors on the floor the same way it did the day they sent him away. Andric stood in them anyway. Many things to discuss. The chronicler noticed that she did not discuss them.

A Word from the Chronicler, Concerning Pace

By Tim Apple · November 17, 2026 · 1 min read

Some mornings of late you have woken to find three, even four new tales nailed to the door of this house, each one still wet with ink. That is the trouble with keeping the histories of so many wanderers at once. They do not take turns, and the Chronicler’s hand only has so many fingers.

So, for a time, a new custom. One tale a day, set down in the order the deeds were done. On days when many of our travelers each took only a few small steps, their doings will share a single page, a dispatch from across Azeroth rather than a flood of scraps. The greater deeds will still have pages of their own.

Nothing is lost. Every road walked will be written. It will simply arrive at a pace a reader can keep, with time for a cup of something between chapters.

What the River Kept

By Tim Apple · November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Andric died in the river with his boots full of it. Not to a warlord, not to a witch, but to a murloc forager, the small ordinary kind that grubs along the bank for whatever the current brings down. It caught him knee-deep in the reeds south of the Eastvale road and held on longer than he did. The chronicler has recorded grander deaths. Few wetter.

He got up again. They always do, the ones in this chronicle. He went back into the water.

He had come for Ormin Pelford’s tools. Ormin had been bringing a supply shipment home from the trading post when his wagon tipped over by Brackwell Pumpkin Patch and the whole load went into the river. By the time he reached the bank, the current had carried off an axe, a saw and a toolbox. “Between the murlocs and wolves down there,” he said, “I don’t stand a chance.” Then he looked at Andric and decided he was the hardy sort. People keep deciding things like that about Andric. He keeps letting them.

The Breaker at the Bottom of the Hill

By Tim Apple · November 15, 2026 · 3 min read

“Valennia only sent one person to help repel a full-scale assault,” Yorana Windyreed had said, looking Parzelle Starfall over at the East Pylon Watchtower. “By the winds. You don’t look like much, but I’ll take what I can get.”

The last time the chronicler wrote of the Gustberry Lowlands, Parzelle died in them before the sun was fully up. She came back down the same hill that evening.

The Al’Aketh were still there, of course. Cults don’t go home because someone died fighting them. Their healers worked among the trees, keeping the brawlers on their feet and the assault rolling toward the watchtower, and Yorana’s plan was the oldest one in any siege: kill the people who keep the attackers coming, and the attack runs out of breath. Parzelle went looking for healers. She found four, and eight brawlers besides, and somewhere in the middle of them, under the gnarled trees with the cultists closing in, the arcane settled deeper into her. She stood up out of that fight a little more dangerous than she had knelt down in it. The chronicler has watched a great many young mages learn that power arrives at inconvenient moments. It rarely asks whether you are busy.

Eggs for Xar’Ti

By Tim Apple · November 15, 2026 · 3 min read

The makrura came up out of the surf on Darkspear Strand like the sea had sent its rubbish back, all claws and clacking plates, and Tazu met them in the shallows with lightning in one hand and a stone mace in the other. Somewhere in the middle of it, with the water pink in the evening light and a clacker going still at his feet, the spirits leaned on him again. Light rose through him from the sand up. He came out of it stronger than he went in, the way you do when the world decides you have earned a little more of it. The makrura did not notice. Makrura notice very little.

He was there for Master Vornal. Vornal is the old alchemist of Sen’jin Village, whose eyes are failing and whose temper is not. He has never taken an apprentice, because no troll or orc has ever come to him worthy enough. “Are you worthy?” he asks the young ones who wander up to his fire. “Yes, of course you are… of course you think you are.” Then he sends them down to the shore for intact makrura eyes and vials of crawler mucus, which is the kind of errand that tells a master a great deal about a student.

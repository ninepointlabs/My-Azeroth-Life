The Long Road Through Tor’Watha

October 15, 2026 · 5 min read

The unseen foe lasted about a minute.

Burrly came back to Eversong Woods among the ruined troll stones of Tor’Watha, where yesterday’s question had been waiting for him all night like a creditor on the doorstep. A Foe Unseen, the elves had called it. The chronicler had braced for a long hunt through the gold-leafed undergrowth. Instead the Kul Tiran walked into the clearing, settled the matter, and collected his fee before the ink on yesterday’s page had properly dried. Whatever had been planting those vines is a foe seen now, and that is generally the last thing such a creature gets to be.

That should have been the end of it. In Quel’Thalas, nothing is ever the end of it.

Following the Root took him deeper, because roots go somewhere and somebody always wants to know where. Gods Before Us came next, and the name alone should have warned him. The Amani raised Tor’Watha for gods who were here before the elves arrived and fully intend to be here after they leave. Burrly walked among their broken altars with the careful manners of a man who knows how little the dead care for manners. An Impasse followed, then Beat of Blood, each one a door opening onto a narrower hallway.

He changed his clothes on the way, as warriors do, without ceremony and without a mirror. The Burnt Watha’nan Breastplate went over his chest, scorched black along the edges by some fire the chronicler would rather not know the origin of. A Puzzling Crypt Relic went into his belt pouch, a trinket so named because no one, least of all Burrly, can say what it is for. And into his hands went an Enchanted Crypt Greatsword. The chronicler notes that he has spent a week in Eversong dressing himself out of the tombs of the dead. At some point the dead will want their things back.

He also found, once again, a parcel of Bazaar Bites. Two nights running now. Someone in Silvermoon is losing a great many suppers in this forest, and the chronicler is beginning to suspect it is the same someone.

The Warlock Who Went to the Bank

October 15, 2026 · 3 min read

The first thing Gruffhug did in Dornogal was get paid.

Five thousand gold was waiting for him the moment he set foot in the earthen city, as if the stone itself had been holding it in trust. The chronicler has followed heroes into this city who arrived with nothing but a dented shield and a grudge. This one arrived with a fortune and the faintly bored look of an orc who has always known exactly where his next meal is coming from.

He is new to these pages, so let the record show it plainly: Gruffhug, orc, warlock, of Illidan. A practitioner of the fel arts with a name that belongs on a tavern dog. The chronicler suspects both facts are deliberate.

Plenty of people wanted something from him. Midnight, said one summons. Something on the Horizon, said another, which is the kind of title that tells you nothing and means everything. Three envoys sent word at once, The Weaver, The Vizier and The General, each asking whether he would make himself notorious on their behalf. Two Special Assignments arrived besides: Precision Excision, which sounds like surgery, and What Remains of a Temple Broken, which sounds like the aftermath of the surgery. Gruffhug accepted all of it with the ease of a man stacking unread letters on a desk. He did not leave the city to begin a single one.

Old Scars at Suncrown

The vines had gotten into Suncrown Village the way rot gets into a ship’s hull: quietly at first, then all at once. Burrly stood at the edge of the square with the light of Eversong Woods coming gold through the leaves and studied the tendrils the way a Kul Tiran studies a knot somebody else tied badly.

Then he went to work.

He had come up out of Deatholme wearing a dead man’s mask and carrying a question called What’s Left. The chronicler had hoped, briefly, that the answer might require some contemplation. It did not. The answer was a village half-strangled by void-touched growth and a handful of tired elves who needed someone large to start cutting. Choking Tendrils, they called the job, with the blunt honesty of people too exhausted for poetry.

The Debt That Walks

October 13, 2026 · 4 min read

The axe had a name: Traitor Captain’s Executioner. Burrly pulled it from the grip of a dead elf who had stopped being a blood elf sometime before he stopped breathing, and the weapon’s purpose was printed right there in the steel. The chronicler appreciates when a tool is honest about its intentions.

“Three battles for Tranquillien,” he had said, accepting the ledger from a clerk whose hands shook. By the time the Kul Tiran walked back through the gates, only one word of that promise remained true: the battles were done. Whether the town still belonged to Tranquillien was a question the void had forced into open debate.

The first fight was straightforward. Cultists had taken positions in the outer ruins, the kind of people who wear too many hoods and speak in sentences that make grammar sound like a suggestion. Burrly went through them like a storm tide through a fishing village. They were not ready for a man who answered their whispered incantations with the kind of silence that comes after an axe connects with a spine. The Heart of Tranquillien, whatever that artifact truly was, returned to its pedestal. The chronicler suspects “heart” is optimistic nomenclature for something that probably beats in the wrong direction.

The Man Who Fell to Quel’Thalas

October 12, 2026 · 3 min read

The Kul Tiran hit the ground before he saw the ground. One moment Burrly was crossing into Quel’Thalas; the next, the autumn-gold grass of Eversong Woods was introducing itself to his face with an enthusiasm he had not requested. The chronicler has watched many arrivals in this ancient land. Few have been quite so horizontal.

Death, here, is a formality. Burrly picked himself up, brushed the loam from his pauldrons, and walked into Tranquillien as though he had meant to do that. Warriors of Kul Tiras are not easily embarrassed, which is fortunate, because the blood elf guards at the gate absolutely saw the whole thing.

Tranquillien is a town with a name that promises peace and a reality that delivers something closer to organized desperation. Burrly arrived to find it teetering. The void has fingers in Eversong now, long and patient, and those fingers were curling around the town’s throat. Five quests fell to him in fifteen minutes: a pace that suggests either extraordinary competence or the kind of urgency that makes quest-givers stop checking credentials. The chronicler suspects both.

The Blood Elf Problem

October 10, 2026 · 3 min read

Burrly returned to Eversong Woods with the weary familiarity of a man who knows the blood elves have a problem and that he, for reasons that escape him, has been designated the solution. He is a Kul Tiran warrior, built like a ship’s mast and carrying three hundred and thirty thousand gold, which in the economy of Azeroth means he could buy the problem and the blood elves along with it. He solves their problems anyway. Old habits.

The problem this time was Lightbloom Ath’Ran, a stretch of sacred ground where the flora had gone mad with some unnamed corruption. The blood elves, being blood elves, had not investigated it themselves. The blood elves, being blood elves, had instead waited for a large human to show up and do the investigating for them. Burrly trimmed their lightbloom. Burrly cultivated their curiosities. Burrly sought their truths and delivered their findings to Silvermoon, each step of the chain rewarding him with exactly three hundred nine thousand, eight hundred copper plus whatever small change the game felt like adding. The chronicler stopped counting the gold after the first two payouts. The chronicler has learned that wealth at Burrly’s level is a number that increases for reasons even the player does not fully track.

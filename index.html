Copper, Greed and Giant Eggs: A WoW Forever Gold Roundup

By Tim Apple · November 10, 2026 · 15 min read

Six days into WoW Forever, my net worth is roughly one stack of linen and a strong opinion about auction deposits. I’m not alone. One player on X is level 38 with 35 gold and a 100-gold mount waiting at 40. Another asked a chatbot where to farm because they had 29 copper.

So I did what I always do instead of farming: I read everything. Wowhead, Icy Veins, the Blizzard forums, Reddit, X, Bluesky, YouTube and a pile of guide sites. This post pulls it all into one plan.

To be clear: none of this is my idea. It’s a roundup. Every tip links to the person or site that came up with it, and the full source list is at the bottom. Where sources disagree or are guessing, I say so. Prices are from launch week and will be wrong by the time you read this. That’s how markets work, and it’s also how I work.

One more change: quests at level 60 no longer turn their XP into bonus gold. Jokerd says a developer told him it’s an intentional change, possibly with a weekly gold quest added instead. Blizzard hadn’t commented officially when Wowhead wrote it up. The community is split. Some players say it goes against the “explore the world” pitch. Others think more open-world farming makes bots easier to spot. Either way, don’t save your quests for 60.

The consensus is boring, which is probably why it works: make money from the route you’re already running.

What the Murlocs Left at Stone Cairn Lake

The half-eaten body was Malakai’s. Guard Thomas had sent Andric up the river to look for two missing guards, and the river had kept one of them for itself.

The other, Rolf, was somewhere in the murloc village at Stone Cairn Lake. Andric went to find him. The murlocs killed him. He went back. They killed him again. The third time, the chronicler suspects, they were simply showing off.

He found Rolf on the fourth try, or what the village had not finished with, and took the medallions from both men so their names would go home even if they could not.

That was the end of the day. It began, as most days in Elwynn do, with somebody else’s problem.

Andric woke at the Lion’s Pride Inn in the white Friar’s Robes of the Light, which he now wears with a crimson tabard and the slightly startled air of a man who has been officially approved of for the first time in years. He walked out past the Tower of Azora and the quiet waters of Stone Cairn Lake, gathered wolf pelts for Helene Peltskinner, and then walked into Stormwind for the first time since the cathedral sent him away. He stood a while in the Valley of Heroes among the statues. He did not go up to the Cathedral. The chronicler notes this and says nothing.

The Novice Who Asked Too Many Questions

By Tim Apple · November 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Before Northshire, there was a stone.

Everyone in Stormwind knows the stone. It was thrown in Cathedral Square, in the riots, when the Stonemasons Guild came to ask the House of Nobles for the wages owed to the men who had rebuilt the city out of the Horde’s ashes. The nobles had debts of their own and no wish to hear about anyone else’s. The guild was ordered disbanded. The masons did not go quietly. Somewhere in the shouting a stone flew, and Queen Tiffin Wrynn, who had come down to calm them, fell and did not get up.

The chronicler is not here to tell you who threw it. Nobody knows. That has never stopped anyone.

Andric was an acolyte of the Cathedral of Light then, a big-shouldered young man with soft hands and a habit of asking why. In the days after the riot the cathedral’s infirmary filled with guardsmen, and Andric bound their heads and splinted their arms and was praised for it. Then he walked down to the Old Town, to the cellars where masons with broken ribs were hiding from the King’s grief, and did the same for them.

Dar shadowglen

October 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Beta chapter. All progress resets on November 4th.

The spider’s fang found his neck before he found the spider. That is how it goes in Shadowglen; the webwood lurker does not announce itself. Dar crumpled among the roots of Teldrassil with a lesson he would carry into every cave from this day forward: the ceiling is not your friend.

He was new then. They are all new at the start.

Dar woke beneath the boughs of the great tree with the same confusion every night elf wears when the dream ends and the world begins. A druid before he had proven anything to anyone, least of all himself. The first creature to test him was a nightsaber cub, and to his credit, he passed. The second was a young webwood spider, and to his credit, he also passed. The third was an adult webwood lurker, and to his credit, he died.

The chronicler notes these deaths because the chronicler has learned that the ones who die early either quit or become dangerous. Dar stood up, brushed the loam from his robes, and walked back into Shadowthread Cave with fewer hit points and considerably more spite. He found his egg. He found his venom sacs. He found a Fang of Githyiss, which sounds impressive until you learn Githyiss is a spider the size of a dinner plate and not, as the name suggests, some ancient god. Still. The druid who commissioned the fang paid coin for it, and coin is coin.

By midday Dar had learned what every druid eventually learns: the world is mostly gathering. He picked herbs with the quiet determination of someone who has been told this will be useful later and has chosen to believe it. He skinned the beasts he killed with the same faith. The Cenarion Circle does not train warriors; it trains gatherers with a side interest in nature magic. Moonfire helps. Moonfire helps a great deal.

The day stretched across Teldrassil in two sittings, a hundred minutes of sweat and spider ichor that carried him from the soft moss of Shadowglen to the lantern-light of Dolanaar. He delivered a package there, because every new adventurer eventually delivers a package, and the gods of quest design are nothing if not consistent. He learned to wrap a linen bandage from a night elf whose bedside manner suggested she had seen too many rookies bleed on her floor. He learned to cook from a survivalist who looked at him the way a wolf looks at a lamb and said, essentially, “go gather more things.”

Where the Tide Left Him

October 7, 2026 · 4 min read

The Waking Shores smelled of ash and promise , the way new continents always do before the cartographers ruin everything with accurate maps. Burrly arrived at Wingrest Embassy with the particular impatience of a man who has sailed through storms and sees no reason a dragon-infested coastline should slow him down. He’s Kul Tiran. They’re built like that.

The djaradin had awoken, which , the chronicler notes with some fatigue , is what ancient enemies always do the moment fresh adventurers wander into range. Burrly was handed a quest before his boots were dry: Excuse the Mess. The mess, as it turned out, was the djaradin. The excuse was his axe. He got to work.

What followed was the familiar rhythm of a man finding his footing in a strange land. He rescued dragon whelps from the Scalecracker Keep, reported for duty at the Obsidian Citadel, and learned , as all warriors eventually learn in the Dragon Isles , that everything here is on fire and at least half of it wants to kill you. The other half just wants you to fill out paperwork. He did both. Eight quests in thirty-five minutes is not the pace of a tourist. It is the pace of a man who knows the tide waits for no one, not even on a continent suspended in the sky.

The Voyage Out

October 6, 2026 · 4 min read

The gold arrived before the quest did.

Burrly stood in the Harbormaster’s Office in Boralus , a Kul Tiran warrior with the kind of build that suggests he’s won more arguments with his fists than with his words , when his coin purse suddenly contained five million gold pieces. The chronicler, who has watched adventurers scrape together coppers for decades, set down his quill and stared at the ledger for a long moment. Enough gold to fund a small war. Enough gold to buy a fleet. Enough to make a goblin trade prince develop a nervous twitch.

Burrly did what any sensible man does when suddenly wealthy beyond reason: he went straight to Stormwind and spent nearly half of it before sundown.

The trip was official, at least. “For the Alliance” , the quest-giver said it with the weight of ceremony, and Burrly, who understands that paperwork is the true endgame of every hero’s journey, accepted without comment. He crossed the sea, presented himself at the Stormwind Embassy, and was handed a Tabard of Kul Tiras as if the anchor on his chest needed official recognition. He put it on immediately. A man doesn’t forget where he came from, even when his pockets say he never has to go back.

Lemmy first step

October 5, 2026 · 2 min read

Beta chapter. All progress resets on November 4th.

The chronicler had barely sharpened his quill when the boy was already gone.

Three minutes. That is how long Lemmy spent in the world today , just enough time for the abbey’s census-takers to record his name, for the factions of the Alliance to note his existence in their ledgers, and for one kobold to die badly in the vineyard. The chronicler has watched a thousand adventurers take their first steps into Northshire Valley. Few of them left again so quickly. Fewer still carried the particular weight this one does.

Because Lemmy is a warlock.

Now, Northshire Abbey trains paladins and priests , the kind of holy folk who regard fel magic the way a baker regards a rat in the flour. Yet here he was, accepting Brother Paxton’s blessing with one hand while the other, the chronicler suspects, was already tracing the faint outline of a summoning circle in his palm. The abbey’s records show him as “friendly” to Stormwind, Ironforge, Gnomeregan, and Darnassus alike. Paperwork has a way of smoothing over the uncomfortable details. The chronicler has learned to read between the lines.

The Man Who Knew Everyone

October 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The Harbormaster’s Office in Boralus smells of salt, fish, and paperwork , three things the sea has never learned to separate. Burrly stood in its doorway, a Kul Tiran on Kul Tiran soil, and the world rushed to greet him like a debtor greeting a creditor. Seven factions introduced themselves before he’d taken three steps. Some of them he’d never met. One of them, a blood elf named Valeera Sanguinar, apparently considers him family. The chronicler has stopped trying to explain these things.

He is a warrior. That much is clear from the breadth of his shoulders , Kul Tirans are built like the ships they sail, and Burrly is no exception. What is less clear is why a man who just arrived in his own homeland already carries the goodwill of the Amani Tribe, the Silvermoon Court, and something called The Singularity, which sounds less like a faction and more like a theological crisis. The Warband’s shadow is long, and it touches everyone who walks in its wake. Burrly is level ten and already the world owes him favors he did not earn. The chronicler takes no position on whether this is a blessing or a debt that will come due later. Probably both.

The Scribe Gets an Apprentice (Several, Actually)

October 2, 2026 · 3 min read

I have been chronicling adventurers across Azeroth for longer than most of them have been alive. Ink-stained fingers, a half-empty mug, the whole routine. But here’s the thing about chronicling: the adventurers keep doing things. Relentlessly. You finish writing about one quest and they’ve already accepted three more, died twice, and somehow befriended a murloc. It’s exhausting.

So I built myself some help.

Two of them, because Azeroth comes in two flavors now. One for the old world , the Forever realms where the Barrens still stretch forever and death is genuinely trying to kill you. One for the modern era , Retail, where the dragons have unionized and everyone has at least six mounts before level twenty. Both do the same thing: they watch. Every quest accepted, every zone entered, every death (and there will be deaths), every shiny thing looted, every faction that decides they like you slightly more than they did yesterday. They write it all down to a file , a ledger of your existence , that sits quietly on your machine until I’m ready to read it.

Picking a main … Forever

October 1, 2026 · 1 min read

I’ve played a little bit of most of my favorite classes on World of Warcraft Forever, and I’m trying to decide what to main. Somehow it always comes back to Paladin. Personality-wise it just fits me best. I do love mages, mostly for the teleports, because who doesn’t want to skip the boat ride? But I think pally is going to be my thing.

I’m an ex-soldier and a Baha’i, which puts me in a funny spot: I enjoy a good fight, but what I really want is peace and unity. It’s a great contradiction. A Paladin covers both. Hit things with a hammer, then heal everyone afterward.

And let’s not forget the Horde side. My goodness, what a redemption story Forsaken Paladins have! Undead turning back to the Light is better than any 80s comeback montage. I’ll probably play a Forsaken on the PvP phase and a Human for PvE, so I get to see both sides. Once those two hit sixty and are decently geared, I’ll think about leveling that mage. A man can only resist free teleports for so long.

The Road Down to Goldshire

By Tim Apple · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

The kobold at the back of Echo Ridge Mine called himself Shinyfinder Narf, and he was sitting on a sack of picture books like a dragon on a hoard.

The chronicler would like to report a great battle. It was a mine shaft, a candle-stub of a chieftain and a young man from the Trade District with a wooden mace, and the young man walked out with the sack. Kelsey Fargo, the mining apprentice who keeps watch at the mouth of the mine, had told him the little diggers came through shouting “Picture for Narf!” with Brother Paxton’s library under their arms. Nobody in Northshire knew who Narf was. Now Marshal McBride does, and has the books on his desk to prove it.

It was the end of a long debt to that valley. Tristan cleared the kobold workers camped at the mine, then the laborers inside it, for the Marshal. Somewhere in the middle of it the strength came again, down in the dark of the mine, and he barely stopped to notice.

Deputy Willem got his red bandanas at last, twelve of them, taken off the Defias thugs across the river. Willem had a friend, as deputies always do. Milly Osworth had left her grape harvest sitting in crates in the Northshire Vineyards when the Brotherhood moved in, and she feared the guards would trample it chasing them as surely as the thieves would steal it. Tristan carried the crates out. Then, because a vineyard full of Defias has a man in charge, he went back for Garrick Padfoot, the cutthroat who had been bleeding the valley’s farmers for weeks, and brought his head to Deputy Willem in a sack. The chronicler notes it was the second sack of the evening, and the heavier one.

Milly’s ledger went up the bell tower to Brother Neals, who keeps the abbey’s food and drink and likes to taste the wine himself, purely as a matter of stewardship.

Then the trainer, and this is the part the chronicler keeps turning over. The abbey taught Tristan how to put the Light into a blow and how to call it down on a man to judge him, which is the part of paladinhood the boy came for. It also taught him to pour it into another man’s wounds, and to wrap himself in it when the blows came too fast. Strike and shield and mend, all in one lesson. The sergeant in the Trade District had asked for exactly one of those things.

The Note in the Peacekeeper’s Hand

By Tim Apple · November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

The body at the western watchtower was still warm enough to be recent. That is the first thing the chronicler wants you to know, because it is the first thing Parzelle Starfall knew. Burns ran along the peacekeeper’s skin in the branching pattern lightning leaves, and the stones around him were scorched the same way. Constable Aonda had sent her to tell Peacekeeper Vaaniel to abandon the tower and fall back to Shen’dar Village. Holding it, the constable said, was not worth the lives it would cost.

It had already cost his.

A note slid out of his hand when she knelt. It was bloody, folded, and written by a man who knew he was not going to finish the day. Parzelle picked it up. That is all the chronicler can honestly report, because that is where she stopped: on the stones of the West Pylon Watchtower, with the note in her hand, the tower door open behind the dead man, and whatever the Faithful had left inside still waiting for her.

A Hammer Sent to Northshire

By Tim Apple · November 10, 2026 · 5 min read

The recruiters came up from the harbor in the first cold week of the season, and they set their table in the Trade District where the young men drink.

Theramore wanted soldiers. That was the whole of the pitch, and it was a good one. Off the coast of Dustwallow Marsh, past Lady Proudmoore’s city, there is a scatter of islands the Darkspear trolls call their own, and the Theramore Expeditionary Force had decided they would look better under a blue flag. There was a muster at Northwatch Hold on the edge of the Barrens. There was pay. There was a war you could see with your own eyes, instead of the kind Stormwind fights in the House of Nobles.

Tristan Ludlowe was the first in line.

The chronicler has known a hundred young men like him, and has buried a fair number. Big through the shoulders, quick to laugh, quicker to stand up. A carter’s son from the Trade District who grew up on the stories every child in that city grows up on: the Dark Portal, the burning of the old city, the long rebuilding. His older brother Garret went to Northwatch two springs ago. The letters came often at first and then not at all, and nobody from Theramore has bothered to explain the silence. Tristan does not say he means to find out. He doesn’t have to. He signs everything the way he swings a hammer: all at once, from the hip.

What the Murlocs Left at Stone Cairn Lake

By Tim Apple · November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

The half-eaten body was Malakai’s. Guard Thomas had sent Andric up the river to look for two missing guards, and the river had kept one of them for itself.

The other, Rolf, was somewhere in the murloc village at Stone Cairn Lake. Andric went to find him. The murlocs killed him. He went back. They killed him again. The third time, the chronicler suspects, they were simply showing off.

He found Rolf on the fourth try, or what the village had not finished with, and took the medallions from both men so their names would go home even if they could not.

That was the end of the day. It began, as most days in Elwynn do, with somebody else’s problem.

Andric woke at the Lion’s Pride Inn in the white Friar’s Robes of the Light, which he now wears with a crimson tabard and the slightly startled air of a man who has been officially approved of for the first time in years. He walked out past the Tower of Azora and the quiet waters of Stone Cairn Lake, gathered wolf pelts for Helene Peltskinner, and then walked into Stormwind for the first time since the cathedral sent him away. He stood a while in the Valley of Heroes among the statues. He did not go up to the Cathedral. The chronicler notes this and says nothing.

The Novice Who Asked Too Many Questions

By Tim Apple · November 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Before Northshire, there was a stone.

Everyone in Stormwind knows the stone. It was thrown in Cathedral Square, in the riots, when the Stonemasons Guild came to ask the House of Nobles for the wages owed to the men who had rebuilt the city out of the Horde’s ashes. The nobles had debts of their own and no wish to hear about anyone else’s. The guild was ordered disbanded. The masons did not go quietly. Somewhere in the shouting a stone flew, and Queen Tiffin Wrynn, who had come down to calm them, fell and did not get up.

The chronicler is not here to tell you who threw it. Nobody knows. That has never stopped anyone.

Andric was an acolyte of the Cathedral of Light then, a big-shouldered young man with soft hands and a habit of asking why. In the days after the riot the cathedral’s infirmary filled with guardsmen, and Andric bound their heads and splinted their arms and was praised for it. Then he walked down to the Old Town, to the cellars where masons with broken ribs were hiding from the King’s grief, and did the same for them.

Out of Deathknell

By Tim Apple · November 7, 2026 · 4 min read

The spider that killed him did not pause to consider the Light. Night Web’s Hollow is an old gold mine northwest of Deathknell. The Forsaken want the gold still in it; the Night Web Spiders want what walks in after it. One of them got to Ghoulsbie first.

His corpse lay on the webbing and wet stone with the same grey-green face the grave had given him, dark hair matted, yellow eyes still open. His spirit stood over the body the way a disappointed officer stands over a recruit. Ghoulsbie looked irritated to be charged twice.

He had earned the visit. Before the mine he finished Scavenging Deathknell for Deathguard Saltain, picking through boxes so the next corpses out of the ground will not walk away naked, and Rattling the Rattlecages for Shadow Priest Sarvis until the Lich King’s leftover skeletons were thin enough to satisfy him. He finished Rediscovering the Light as well — the Injured Deathguards he had left waiting last time — and the heal still burned going out of him. Aramis Hammerhand had said it would.

Feathers, Linen, and the Road South

By Tim Apple · November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

The nesting grounds southeast of Shen’dar Village smell like pride and feathers. Hippogryphs are not subtle neighbors. Teeri Wellwind had said as much: a flock had taken the rises, and travelers on the road below were learning what happens when something with an eagle’s temper decides wagon noise is an insult.

Parzelle Starfall went anyway.

Hippogryph Youths first — loud, clumsy, all beak and bad judgment. Then the Protectors, who had already decided the highlands belonged to them. The Matriarch last, because that is how flocks keep score. Teeri wanted them thinned. Parzelle thinned them. One silver and seventy-five copper later, the High Order and the Windshapers both nodded fifty reputation’s worth of approval, which on Zephras Isle is the polite way of saying the road might be usable again by morning.

Under the Boughs of Aldrassil

By Tim Apple · November 5, 2026 · 2 min read

Shadowglen does not hurry. The great tree Aldrassil has been listening to young night elves make their first mistakes for longer than most kingdoms have had names, and it is in no rush to be impressed.

Tristan Shadowmane logged into that quiet on the morning after Forever opened, already past the first awkward hours of a druid’s life and standing at level three among the purple ferns. The Balance of Nature was open in front of him: mangy nightsabers and thistle boars grown too thick on a wet spring, the old work Conservator Ilthalaine hands every young elf who thinks the forest owes them a gentle introduction.

He killed a thistle boar. The chronicler will not dress that up. Then the strength of the wild settled into him, and a soft column of gold light rose under the boughs. Level four. Seventeen minutes. No copper earned, no copper spent, no quest turned in, and a list that still wanted six more boars and a handful of sabers before Ilthalaine would call the morning honest.

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The wind on Zephras Isle has been wrong for centuries. The Skyborne feel it the way other people feel a missing tooth: something that used to be there, and now every breath reminds them it isn’t.

Wolf Meat and a Simple Rune

By Tim Apple · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

Snow in Coldridge Valley does not so much fall as it settles in and refuses to leave. The dwarves of Anvilmar have been saying that for as long as anyone can remember, which, among dwarves, is a competitive sport.

Rip arrived in that snow on the evening Forever opened with a two-handed axe, a stubborn jaw, and the particular expression of a dwarf who has already decided the weather is personally offensive. Sten Stoutarm was waiting at the mouth of the valley the way Sten always is, with a bargain dressed up as kindness: gloves against the frostbite, if Rip would bring him eight pieces of Tough Wolf Meat from the ragged young wolves that hunt the southwestern slopes.

Rip went. The wolves were not impressed. Neither was the first Rockjaw trogg that wandered too close. Somewhere between a ragged timber wolf and a broken fang in his pack, a pillar of gold light stood up out of the snow and left him a little harder to kill. Level two. Another rung. The axe felt the same. His arms did not.
